# False Morels

## True morels vs false morels
Morel mushrooms (Morchella species) are highly prized edible mushrooms with a distinctive honeycomb cap. False morels (Gyromitra species, especially Gyromitra esculenta) look superficially similar to inexperienced foragers but contain monomethylhydrazine (MMH), a toxin chemically similar to rocket fuel. People die from false morel poisoning every year, mostly from eating dried-and-rehydrated specimens believed to be safe.

## Identification — the brain test
**True morel:** cap is honeycomb-like, with regular vertical and horizontal pits and ridges. Cap is hollow when cut lengthwise — completely empty from inside. Stem attaches to the cap base — they fuse smoothly. Color usually tan, gray, or black-brown.

**False morel:** cap looks "brain-like" or "saddle-like" — wavy, lobed, irregular folds rather than regular honeycomb pits. Cap is NOT hollow — there's a wad of cottony fiber inside, or solid flesh. Cap is loose on the stem (just sits on top), not fused. Color usually reddish-brown, dark brown, or yellowish.

When you cut a suspect "morel" lengthwise from cap top to stem base and it's not completely hollow, you have a false morel.

## Toxicity
MMH causes vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness, headache within 6–12 hours. Severe cases progress to liver damage, kidney damage, hemolytic anemia (red blood cell destruction), and seizures. Some species are more toxic than others, but identification at the species level is difficult — treat all "false morels" as toxic.

## The cooking myth
Folk tradition holds that boiling false morels in water (and discarding the water) detoxifies them. The toxin is volatile and can be partially removed this way. However: residual toxin levels are unpredictable, the steam itself can poison people doing the cooking (well-ventilated only — and even then, fatalities have occurred), and some individuals are sensitive to even small residual amounts. People who eat "properly prepared" false morels have died.

## Dried false morels are NOT safer
A common misconception: drying false morels reduces MMH. It does, somewhat — but unevenly, unpredictably, and people have died from rehydrated dried false morels. Commercial sales of dried Gyromitra are banned in many countries. If a forager dies eating "dried morels," it was almost certainly a Gyromitra confused with the real thing.

## What to do
Any suspected false morel ingestion warrants emergency care. Call Poison Control. Symptoms appearing 6–24 hours after eating "morels" should be treated as poisoning, not as ordinary GI illness. Treatment is supportive plus methylene blue for hemolysis if it develops; vitamin B6 in large doses can help. Liver and kidney function need monitoring.

## Safe morel foraging
Morels are one of the more "beginner-safe" mushrooms IF you learn proper identification from an experienced person in your region. Memorize the hollow-cap-completely-empty-inside test. When in doubt, throw it out. New foragers should bring every harvest to an experienced person for confirmation until they have multiple successful seasons.
