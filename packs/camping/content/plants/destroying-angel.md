# Destroying Angel

## A pure-white killer
Destroying angels are a group of pure-white Amanita species (A. virosa, A. bisporigera in the East, A. ocreata in the West) that contain the same amatoxins as the death cap. Pretty, white, looks innocent — and lethal. They grow across the US in mixed and hardwood forests. Like other Amanitas, the toxin survives cooking, drying, and freezing.

## Identification
Cap 2–5 inches across, pure white throughout (occasionally with very faint cream tints). Smooth surface, sometimes sticky in wet weather. Cap is dome-shaped young, flattening with age. Gills white, free from stem. Stem white, 3–6 inches, with a white ring (annulus) and a prominent white cup (volva) at the base — often deep in soil and easily missed if you don't dig it up. White spore print. Often grows scattered or in small groups.

## Why it's confused with edibles
Inexperienced foragers mistake destroying angels for:
- **Button mushrooms / store-bought white mushrooms** (Agaricus bisporus): button mushroom has pink-to-brown gills as it matures, brown spore print, NO ring, NO volva.
- **Meadow mushroom** (Agaricus campestris): pink gills, no volva.
- **Horse mushroom** (Agaricus arvensis): white when young but gills turn pink then brown; no volva.

The key check: ALWAYS dig up the base. If the mushroom has a sheath / cup at the base, it's potentially Amanita. White Amanitas with rings and volvas are deadly. There's no folk rule for telling them apart from edibles by smell, taste, or behavior — only the physical features.

## Amatoxin poisoning
Same toxin profile as death cap (see plants.death-cap): three-stage poisoning with a deceptive "honeymoon" period of apparent recovery in stage 2. Treatment is intensive liver and kidney support, with liver transplant for worst cases. Even with optimal treatment, mortality is significant — often 10–30% depending on how quickly care is started.

## What to do
Any suspected destroying angel ingestion is an emergency, regardless of how mild the initial symptoms seem. Call Poison Control immediately. Get to a hospital that can handle hepatotoxic poisoning. Bring the mushroom and any leftovers. Activated charcoal early can help. N-acetylcysteine, silibinin if available, and aggressive supportive care give best outcomes.

## Children and dogs
A particular danger pattern: white mushrooms in a yard or campsite tempt children and dogs. White mushroom in lawn = treat as potentially deadly. Remove visible mushrooms from camp areas where kids play. If a dog has eaten a white mushroom, contact a veterinarian immediately — dogs metabolize amatoxin similarly to humans and the same liver-failure pattern follows.

## Universal Amanita rule
Memorize this for life: any mushroom with a ring on the stem AND a cup or sheath at the base is potentially deadly. Many edible mushrooms have neither. The handful of Amanitas that ARE edible (Caesar's mushroom, blusher) are NEVER worth the risk for amateurs — the cost of being wrong is liver failure, kidney failure, and death. Stick to species with no deadly look-alikes if you forage at all.
