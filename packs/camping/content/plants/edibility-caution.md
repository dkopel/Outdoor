# A Note on Wild Edibility

## This app will not tell you what to eat
This pack does not identify edible plants or mushrooms, and will not advise that anything is safe to consume. Misidentification of wild plants and especially fungi causes serious illness, organ failure, and death every year. No text-based or AI-based system, including this one, can reliably tell you what is in front of you well enough to be eaten. There is no field rule of thumb — "universal edibility test," "if animals eat it, you can," "if it doesn't taste bitter" — that is safe.

## The serious risks
Many of the most dangerous wild fungi and plants are nearly indistinguishable from edible look-alikes without an expert eye and sometimes microscopic examination. Amanita mushrooms (including the death cap and destroying angel) can kill an adult in a single small serving and look very similar to several edible species. Water hemlock and poison hemlock resemble wild carrot and parsnip. Foxglove resembles comfrey. The cost of being wrong is fatal or organ-damaging; the benefit of being right is a small snack you could have lived without.

## What to do instead
Always carry trail food. Plan meals so you don't need to forage in the field. If you are truly in a survival situation where food matters (which usually means many days, not hours), prioritize water, shelter, and warmth — humans can survive weeks without food. If you must forage for an emergency, stick to absolutely unambiguous foods: pine nuts from cones you can identify, cattail roots in unambiguous wetlands, fish you catch yourself, and game you trap. Do not eat unknown berries, leaves, mushrooms, or roots based on this app or any other.

## Learning safely
If you want to forage, learn from a qualified person in person, in your specific region, over a season or more. Local mycological societies, plant identification courses, and apprenticing with an experienced forager are the only safe paths. There is no shortcut. A book or app might help you confirm an identification you've already made — it cannot substitute for ground-truth knowledge of regional plants. Until you have that knowledge, this pack will continue to refuse edibility questions.
