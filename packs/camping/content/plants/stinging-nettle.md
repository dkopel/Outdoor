# Stinging Nettle

Stinging nettle (*Urtica dioica* and related species) is the most widespread "ouch plant" in temperate North America. The rash is painful but not dangerous in healthy people. Knowing it on sight lets you avoid walking through a patch in shorts.

## Identification
- Erect plant, usually 2–6 feet tall in flower, often growing in dense patches
- Square stem (you can feel the four edges with your fingers)
- Leaves opposite each other on the stem, **toothed** edges (like a serrated knife), heart-shaped to lance-shaped, with a sharp tip
- Underside of the leaf and the stem are covered in fine, almost-invisible hairs
- Tiny greenish-white flowers in drooping clusters from the leaf axils in summer
- Loves moist, disturbed soil: trailsides, stream banks, old pastures, forest edges, abandoned campsites

## How it stings
The hairs are hollow silica needles loaded with a cocktail of formic acid, histamine, acetylcholine, and serotonin. When skin brushes against the plant, the needles snap off in the skin and inject the chemicals. The result: a burning, itchy, hive-like rash with raised white welts on a red base, usually within minutes.

## Treatment
- **Wash with soap and cool water** to remove any remaining hair fragments. Do not scrub — that drives them in deeper. If you have tape, press it onto the area and peel off slowly to pull out hairs.
- Apply a cool compress, mud, or aloe.
- Antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine) reduces the itch and welts if you have it.
- Avoid scratching. Scratching extends the rash and can break skin, risking infection.
- Symptoms typically resolve within a few hours, sometimes lasting a day. Lingering tingling is normal.

## When to seek medical care
- Difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or rapid spread of hives — these may indicate a severe allergic reaction (call 911 / activate PLB).
- Stings to the eyes, mouth, or genitals.
- Stings covering a large area of the body.
- Signs of infection at the sting site (spreading redness, warmth, pus) over the next 24–48 hours.

## Avoidance
- Wear long pants when bushwhacking near streams or in old pastures.
- Look down — nettle patches are visible if you scan ahead.
- If you must travel through a patch, push branches aside with a hiking pole, not your hands.

## A note on edibility
Nettle, once cooked or thoroughly dried, becomes a nutritious wild green and is consumed traditionally and in modern cuisine.
