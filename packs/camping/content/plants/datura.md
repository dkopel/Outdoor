# Datura (Jimsonweed)

## Why it kills
Datura contains tropane alkaloids (atropine, scopolamine, hyoscyamine) that affect the nervous system and heart. People are poisoned three ways: eating berries or seeds (kids find them attractive), brewing tea or smoking dried plant material recreationally for hallucinogenic effects (deaths from this are common — the toxic dose is unpredictable), and accidental contact with contaminated food. Datura's hallucinations are not pleasant — most who survive describe them as terrifying.

## Identification
An annual plant 2–5 feet tall with large, jagged-edged oval leaves that smell unpleasant when crushed. Flowers are large, trumpet-shaped, 3–4 inches long, white or pale lavender, opening in the evening. Fruit is a spiny round capsule about the size of a walnut, containing many small black-brown seeds. Found throughout the US in disturbed soil — roadsides, abandoned fields, pastures, compost piles. Closely related species (Datura inoxia, Datura wrightii) look similar and are equally toxic.

## Symptoms
The classic toxicology mnemonic for tropane alkaloid poisoning: "blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, red as a beet, hot as a hare, dry as a bone." Dilated pupils, blurred vision, severe agitation and hallucinations, flushed skin, high fever, dry mouth and skin (cannot sweat). Also: rapid heart rate, urinary retention, seizures, coma. Onset is 30 minutes to several hours after ingestion. Effects can persist 24–48 hours.

## What to do
Evacuate immediately to emergency care. Call Poison Control. The patient may be combative, paranoid, or having terrifying hallucinations — keep them safe from self-injury. Cool the body (cool wet cloths, fan) to manage fever. Treatment is mostly supportive — sedation, fluids, sometimes physostigmine antidote for severe cases. Activated charcoal early can reduce absorption.

## Recreational use is a major hazard
Datura is sometimes sought out for hallucinogenic effects. Internet folklore suggests "safe doses" — none exist. The toxic concentration varies wildly between plants, parts of the same plant, and even season. Hospital-documented deaths and severe permanent injuries from "recreational" Datura use are common, especially among teenagers. This is one of the most predictably-fatal recreational drugs and one of the most preventable poisonings.

## Don't grow it as ornamental
Some Datura species (like Angel's Trumpet, Brugmansia, which is related) are sold as garden plants because of their dramatic flowers. They are equally toxic. Children eating the seeds is a recurring poisoning pattern. If you have small children or visitors with children, remove these plants from your garden.
