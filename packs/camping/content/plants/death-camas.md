# Death Camas

## Why it kills foragers
Death camas (Zigadenus, sometimes Toxicoscordion) looks similar to wild onions and edible camas lily — both used historically as food by Native peoples. It contains zygacine, a steroidal alkaloid that affects the heart and nerves. As little as a single bulb has killed adults. It grows across most of North America: prairies, meadows, foothills, and rocky slopes from sea level to alpine.

## The key identification difference
**Wild onion has the unmistakable onion or garlic smell when crushed. Death camas does NOT.** Always crush a leaf and sniff before considering any onion-like bulb. No onion smell = do not eat. Death camas leaves are grass-like, V-shaped in cross-section, growing in a basal cluster. Flowers are small, cream-white or greenish, in upright clusters or open panicles at the top of an 1–3 foot stem. Bulbs look like small onions — that's the deadly resemblance.

## Edible camas vs death camas
Edible camas (Camassia quamash) has BLUE or violet flowers; death camas has white-cream flowers. But they often grow in the same meadows, and the bulbs are nearly identical. Native foragers traditionally harvested camas only when in bloom so they could see the flower color. Outside flowering season, identification by bulb alone is unreliable — even experienced foragers have died.

## Symptoms
Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain within 1–3 hours of eating. Salivation, weakness, dizziness, slow heart rate, low blood pressure. Severe cases progress to coma and death from cardiovascular collapse. Symptoms can be delayed up to 8 hours, so "feeling fine" after eating an unknown bulb is not reassurance.

## If exposure
Evacuate to emergency care immediately. Call Poison Control. Bring the plant for identification. Treatment is supportive — IV fluids, cardiac monitoring, atropine for slow heart rate, sometimes mechanical ventilation. There is no antidote. Children and small adults are at higher risk for fatal outcomes.

## The general rule
This pack will not identify any plant as safe to eat. The death camas / wild onion confusion has killed careful, informed people. Both grow in beautiful mountain meadows alongside abundant safer food. Do not eat any bulb without expert in-person identification. Do not eat any grass-like plant whose bulb you can't smell as clearly onion or garlic.
