# Water Hemlock

## Often called the most toxic plant in North America
Water hemlock (Cicuta maculata, C. douglasii, and related species) is among the most violently toxic plants on the continent. A single bite of the root can kill an adult within hours. The toxin (cicutoxin) causes violent seizures that often progress to death from respiratory failure or status epilepticus. There is no antidote — only supportive ICU care. The plant grows across nearly the entire US, especially in wet meadows, marshes, stream banks, and ditches.

## Identification
2–6 feet tall (smaller than poison hemlock). Hollow stems often streaked or splotched purple at the base. Leaves pinnately compound, sharply toothed, with veins ending in the NOTCHES between teeth rather than in the teeth themselves — a useful identification mark. Small white flowers in umbrella-shaped clusters. The most diagnostic feature is the root: cut a root open lengthwise — you'll see distinct chambers separated by horizontal partitions. No other plant in the carrot family has this. Roots smell like raw parsnip or carrot, dangerously seductive.

## Why it kills people
Roots look and smell almost exactly like wild parsnip, wild carrot (Queen Anne's lace), or wild celery. Foragers digging "wild carrots" near streams have repeatedly died. The toxin works fast — symptoms within 15–60 minutes. By the time someone realizes they've made a mistake, treatment options are very limited. Children grabbing the hollow stem to make whistles have been poisoned by skin contact and chewing.

## Symptoms
Excessive salivation and sweating, abdominal cramps, vomiting. Then violent muscle twitching escalating to full seizures, often within an hour. Breathing failure during seizures is the most common cause of death. Unlike poison hemlock (which causes ascending paralysis), water hemlock causes convulsions.

## What to do
If anyone is suspected of eating or chewing water hemlock, evacuate immediately to emergency medical care. Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222 in the US). Do not induce vomiting unless directed. Bring a sample of the plant — root, stem, leaves — for identification. Time is critical; seizures can begin within 30 minutes. Even small ingestions are emergencies.

## Avoiding it
The simplest rule: do not eat any wild plant from the carrot family (Apiaceae) without expert in-person identification. The carrot family contains both edibles (carrot, parsnip, celery, fennel, anise, parsley, dill) and deadly species (water hemlock, poison hemlock, fool's parsley). Even experienced foragers misidentify these plants. If you're in wet ground anywhere in the US and see white umbrella-shaped flowers on a hollow-stemmed plant — stay away.
