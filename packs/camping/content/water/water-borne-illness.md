# Water-Borne Illness

## Giardia
Giardia is a single-celled parasite present in most North American surface water, including high alpine streams. Symptoms typically appear 1–3 weeks after exposure: foul-smelling diarrhea, gas, abdominal cramps, nausea, fatigue, weight loss. Usually self-limiting but can persist for weeks without treatment. Giardia cysts are killed by boiling and removed by 0.2-micron filters. Chemical treatments work but need longer contact times in cold water — read the dose chart carefully.

## Cryptosporidium ("Crypto")
A tougher parasite than giardia — chemical treatment is far less effective. Symptoms similar to giardia: watery diarrhea, cramps, nausea, fever, dehydration, appearing 2–10 days after exposure. Boiling kills crypto. Filters rated to 1 micron or less remove it physically. Chlorine dioxide tablets work but typically need 4 hours of contact time at full concentration. Chlorine and iodine do not kill crypto reliably. If the water might contain livestock or human contamination, filter and chemically treat, or boil.

## E. coli and other bacteria
E. coli, Salmonella, Campylobacter, and Shigella cause vomiting, fever, watery or bloody diarrhea, often within 1–3 days. These are typically from water contaminated by feces (human, livestock, beaver). Boiling, filtration to 0.2 microns, chemical treatment, and UV all work against bacteria. Most cases in healthy adults resolve in 3–7 days. Watch for signs requiring evacuation: bloody stool, persistent fever, severe dehydration, weakness.

## Viruses
Hepatitis A, norovirus, rotavirus, and other waterborne viruses are small enough to pass through 0.2-micron filters. Boiling, chemical treatment (chlorine dioxide, iodine), and UV inactivate them. Viral contamination is rare in clear North American backcountry water but common in heavily-used areas (popular trails, near agriculture, in much of the world). For travel or heavily-used areas, combine filtration with chemical or boil.

## Algae and cyanobacteria toxins
Blue-green algae blooms in warm still water (lakes, ponds) can produce toxins that no filter or chemical removes — boiling concentrates them rather than removing them. Skip lakes that look bright green, have a paint-like sheen, or where the water smells musty/swampy. Dogs that swim and drink in such lakes can die quickly; humans get GI illness, rashes, sometimes nerve effects. If a water source looks or smells wrong, find a different source.
