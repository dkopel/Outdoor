# Water Purification Methods

## Boiling
Boiling is the most reliable single method for inactivating pathogens (bacteria, viruses, protozoa) in clear water. Bring the water to a rolling boil for at least 1 minute at sea level, or 3 minutes above approximately 6,500 feet (2,000 meters). The water does not need to keep boiling — reaching a rolling boil and holding it briefly is enough. Boiling does not remove chemical contaminants, sediment, salt, or heavy metals; pre-filter cloudy water through a clean cloth and let it settle before boiling for best results.

## Filtration
A camping-grade filter rated to remove particles of 0.2 microns or smaller removes bacteria and protozoa (Giardia, Cryptosporidium) but generally does not remove viruses. In North America and most temperate backcountry environments, viral contamination of clear water is rare, and a 0.2-micron filter is adequate. For travel water or water heavily contaminated by humans/livestock, combine filtration with chemical treatment or boiling. Replace filter cartridges per manufacturer specs; a torn or compromised filter is not a filter.

## Chemical treatment
Chlorine dioxide tablets, iodine tablets, and unscented household bleach are the field-practical chemical options. Chlorine dioxide is effective against viruses, bacteria, and Cryptosporidium but typically needs a 4-hour wait for Crypto-killing concentrations. Iodine is effective against bacteria and viruses but not Cryptosporidium, and is not safe for pregnant people, people with thyroid issues, or prolonged use. Always follow the dose and wait time on the package. Cold and turbid water requires longer contact times — double the wait if water is colder than ~5°C or visibly cloudy.

## UV light
Handheld UV purifiers (e.g., SteriPen-type devices) inactivate bacteria, viruses, and protozoa in clear water with a few minutes of exposure. They require clear water (turbidity blocks the UV) and working batteries. Pre-filter cloudy water through a cloth or pump filter first. UV doesn't remove dead organisms or chemicals; it only stops them reproducing — but pathogens that can't reproduce can't make you sick.

## When to combine methods
For the highest confidence — heavy contamination, immunocompromised users, travel to areas with high viral load — combine pre-filtration (cloth or fine filter) with either boiling or chemical disinfection. For everyday backcountry use in clear North American streams, a single quality 0.2-micron filter or boiling is generally sufficient. Sediment-heavy water always benefits from settling and pre-filtering before any other treatment.
