# If You Become Lost

## STOP — Stop, Think, Observe, Plan
The first response to "I might be lost" is STOP. Stop moving. Sit down. Drink water. Think — when did you last know exactly where you were, and what direction have you been walking since? Observe — what landmarks can you see? Plan — do not move until you have a plan with a real reason behind it. People get into terminal trouble by walking faster when uncertain; calm clear thinking from a sitting position fixes most "lost" situations.

## Stay put or self-rescue
If you've told someone your trip plan and expected return, staying put is usually safer than moving. Searchers start at the last known point and work outward; if you keep moving, they may have to re-search areas already cleared. Stay where you are, get warm and dry, hydrate, build a fire if conditions allow, and make yourself easy to find. Move only if you're certain you can reach a known feature (a road, river, marked trail) without making things worse.

## Active signaling
Three of anything is the international distress signal: three whistle blasts, three shots, three smoky signal fires arranged in a triangle, three blinks of a mirror. Carry a real whistle — it works far longer than your voice. A signal mirror (or any reflective surface) can flash a pilot from 10+ miles in clear conditions; aim by holding the mirror up and sighting through the small hole at the target while flashing back the bright spot on your other hand. Spread out bright clothing or gear on open ground or snow for aerial spotting.

## PLBs and satellite messengers
A Personal Locator Beacon (PLB) or satellite messenger (inReach, Spot, Zoleo) is the most reliable rescue signal in serious backcountry. Activate it from open sky if possible, give it 5–15 minutes to acquire satellites and transmit, and stay near the activation point. PLBs are one-shot SOS devices with no two-way communication; satellite messengers allow texting which lets rescuers know your condition. Carry one for solo trips, expeditions, or any trip beyond reliable cell coverage.
