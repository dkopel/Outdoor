# Square Knot (Reef Knot)

The square knot is one of the oldest and most common knots, used for joining two ropes of equal diameter under light load. It is excellent for tying off bandages, joining shoelaces, and securing non-critical bundles. It is **not** appropriate for any life-safety or high-load application — it can slip or capsize under uneven load and is dangerous in climbing or rescue contexts. For joining ropes that will bear weight, use a sheet bend, double fisherman's, or figure-eight bend instead.

## How to tie
1. Take the two rope ends, one in each hand.
2. Lay the right end over the left end and tuck it under, forming a half-knot. (Right over left, then under.)
3. Now lay the left end over the right end and tuck it under, forming a second half-knot. (Left over right, then under.)
4. Dress (pull each strand snug) and tighten.

The mnemonic is "right over left, then left over right" — the knot's two halves mirror each other, which is what makes it a square knot rather than a granny knot.

## Recognize a correct square knot
A correctly tied square knot has both working ends emerging on the **same** side of the standing parts — both top, or both bottom. If the ends emerge on opposite sides (one top, one bottom), you've tied a granny knot by mistake. Granny knots slip, jam, and are unreliable. Untie and retie.

## Best uses
- Tying off a triangular bandage or roller bandage in first aid (the flat profile is comfortable on skin)
- Tying a bundle of sticks or gear together for carry
- Joining shoelaces or short cordage of equal size
- Closing a bag mouth
- Reefing a sail (the original "reef knot" name)

## Where NOT to use it
- Connecting climbing ropes (use a double fisherman's or flat overhand bend)
- Bearing significant load (it slips and jams under heavy or uneven pull)
- Joining ropes of unequal diameter (use a sheet bend)
- Any life-safety situation

## Untying
The square knot releases easily by collapsing one side: grip both working ends on one side and push them toward the standing parts. The knot loosens and falls apart. This is one of its useful properties for bandaging — easy on, easy off when needed.

This is a low-stakes utility knot. For anything carrying weight or holding a life, use a knot rated for that purpose.
