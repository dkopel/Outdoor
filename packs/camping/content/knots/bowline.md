# Bowline

## What it does
The bowline makes a fixed loop at the end of a rope that does not slip or tighten under load — useful for tying around a tree, around your waist in a self-rescue, or to a fixed point. It can be untied easily even after heavy loading, which is why it's a favorite outdoor and rescue knot. The bowline is not a climbing tie-in by modern standards; use the figure-eight follow-through for that.

## How to tie it (rabbit hole method)
1. With the working end, form a small loop in the standing line (the "rabbit hole"). The standing line is the long, fixed end; the working end is the short tail you're moving.
2. Pass the working end up through the small loop ("the rabbit comes out of the hole").
3. Bring the working end around behind the standing line ("around the tree").
4. Pass the working end back down through the small loop ("and back into the hole").
5. Hold the working end and the new big loop, and pull the standing line to dress and tighten.

## Common mistakes
The most common error is reversing the direction of step 2, which produces a "left-handed" bowline that is significantly weaker and prone to capsizing under cyclic loading. Always leave a tail at least 6 inches (15 cm) long after dressing the knot. For safety-critical applications, finish the bowline with a backup half hitch on the standing line — this prevents the knot from working loose if cycled. A bowline alone can shake loose when slack/load cycles repeatedly.

## When not to use it
Do not use a plain bowline for life-safety climbing tie-in — use the figure-eight follow-through. Avoid the bowline on stiff or slick modern ropes without a backup; it can slip. For loads that need to be repeatedly tensioned and released, a taut-line hitch or trucker's hitch is the right tool.
