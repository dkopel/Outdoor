# Trucker's Hitch

## What it does
The trucker's hitch creates a 2:1 (or 3:1 with a friction loss caveat) mechanical advantage system for tensioning a line — useful for cinching a tarp ridgeline tight, lashing a kayak or load to a roof rack, or pulling a guyline so taut it could play a note. With basic cord and zero hardware, it lets one person tension a line beyond what they could pull bare-handed.

## How to tie it
1. Anchor one end of the rope (e.g., bowline around a tree, or a fixed object).
2. About a third of the way down from the load end, tie a slipped overhand or a "marlinspike loop" to create a loop in the standing line. This is your pulley substitute.
3. Pass the working end around your second anchor (the object you're tying off to).
4. Bring the working end back up through the loop you formed in step 2.
5. Pull down on the working end — this gives you mechanical advantage. Pull until tension is where you want it.
6. Lock off with two half hitches around the doubled lines below the loop, or a slipped half hitch for quick release.

## Friction matters
The theoretical advantage is 2:1, but in practice cord-on-cord friction in the loop eats much of that. Use a smooth carabiner or a small pulley as the directional element if you have one — that recovers most of the lost efficiency and makes the system smoother to release. On slick line, the slipped overhand loop in step 2 can roll out under high tension — back it up with a real fixed loop (figure-eight on a bight) if the load will be high.

## When you need it
Anytime tightening by hand isn't enough: pitching a flat tarp in heavy weather, stabilizing a hammock, lashing gear on a vehicle, or tensioning a clothesline you actually want to be a clothesline. Pair it with a taut-line hitch on the other end of the line for a fully adjustable system.
