# Sheet Bend

The sheet bend joins two ropes together, especially ropes of **different diameters or materials**. It is more secure than a square knot under unequal load and is the standard "join two ropes" knot in non-climbing applications. A double sheet bend (one extra wrap) is even more secure and is the version to use when you cannot supervise the knot under load.

## How to tie (single sheet bend)
1. Take the thicker (or stiffer) rope and form a bight — a U-shape — with the working end pointing back along the rope.
2. Pass the thinner rope's working end up through the bight from underneath.
3. Pass it around behind both legs of the bight.
4. Tuck the working end back under itself — under the part you just brought around — but **on the same side of the bight** as the standing part of the thinner rope.
5. Dress and tighten by pulling on the standing parts.

A correctly tied sheet bend has both working ends emerging on the **same side** of the knot. If they are on opposite sides, it is a less secure variation and may slip.

## Double sheet bend
For slippery rope, large diameter differences, or unattended loads:
- After step 3, wrap the working end around the bight a **second** time before tucking under in step 4.
- Two wraps significantly increase friction and security.

## Best uses
- Joining a thin rope to a thicker one (e.g., extending a tarp guyline)
- Joining synthetic to natural fiber rope
- Joining ropes when neither end is anchored yet
- Field bandage or cord repairs
- Attaching a line to a closed loop (lanyard, becket, fixed eye)

## Cautions
- Sheet bends can shake loose under cyclic load. For loads that pulse (wind on a tarp, repeated tug), use the double sheet bend and leave long tails.
- Not for climbing. For climbing ropes, use a flat overhand bend (EDK) or double fisherman's.
- The thicker rope is always the one with the bight; the thinner rope is the one that does the weaving. Reversing causes the knot to slip.

## Untying
Even after heavy load, a sheet bend can usually be broken by working the bight loose from the standing rope and rolling the wraps off. Less prone to jamming than a square knot under load.

The sheet bend is the workhorse "join" knot for general camp use. Learn it once and you'll use it for the rest of your outdoor career.
