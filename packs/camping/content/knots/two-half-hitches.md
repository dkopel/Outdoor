# Two Half Hitches, Sheet Bend, Square Knot

## Two half hitches
A reliable way to tie a rope to a post or ring when the load is steady. Wrap the working end around the object, then around the standing line, and tuck through (one half hitch). Repeat the same direction (the second half hitch) and dress. Two half hitches in the same direction is more secure than alternating. Easy to tie under tension and easy to untie. Back it up with a third half hitch if the load will cycle a lot.

## Sheet bend (joining two ropes)
Use a sheet bend to join two ropes, especially when they are different diameters. Form a bight (U-shape) in the thicker rope. Pass the thinner rope's working end up through the bight, around behind both legs of the bight, and back under itself (not through the bight again). Tighten so the working end is on the same side as the working end of the bight — that's the secure version. Mismatched-side ("left-handed") sheet bends are weaker. For slick or stiff line, double the wraps (double sheet bend).

## Square knot (reef knot) — and what it isn't
A square knot binds the ends of two ropes of equal diameter around something — most commonly to tie off a bundle, secure a bandage, or lash gear. To tie: right over left, then left over right (the mantra). The square knot is **not** safe for joining ropes that will bear load — it can capsize and slip. Use a sheet bend or a double fisherman's for joining loads.

## When to use what
- Tying a rope to a tree or post: two half hitches, or a bowline if you want a fixed loop.
- Joining two ropes for tension (clothesline, ridgeline extension): sheet bend.
- Joining two ropes for life-safety: not in this pack — take a class.
- Bundling firewood, securing a roll: square knot.
