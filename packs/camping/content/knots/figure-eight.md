# Figure-Eight Family

## The plain figure-eight (stopper knot)
A figure-eight tied near the end of a rope keeps the rope from passing through a hole — a stopper. To tie it: form a loop, pass the working end behind the standing line, then around and through the loop. Pull tight. The knot is easier to untie after loading than an overhand, which is why climbers prefer it. Always leave a tail of at least 6 inches (15 cm).

## Figure-eight on a bight
For a fixed loop in the middle of a rope, fold the rope back on itself to make a "bight," then tie a figure-eight with the doubled rope. Dress carefully so the strands lie parallel without crossing. This knot is very strong and easy to untie, useful for clipping into a midpoint with a carabiner.

## Figure-eight follow-through
The follow-through is how climbers tie in. Start a single figure-eight a few feet from the rope end, pass the working end through the harness tie-in points, then re-trace the figure-eight back along the original. Result: a figure-eight on a bight around the harness. Dress so the strands lie flat and parallel — never crossed. Finish with a backup knot (a barrel/double overhand) snug against the figure-eight. If you're using this for climbing, take a class — written instructions are no substitute.

## When to use which
- Stopper at the end of a rope: plain figure-eight (or double overhand).
- Loop in the middle of a rope: figure-eight on a bight.
- Tying to a harness, ring, or fixed point: figure-eight follow-through.
- Where strength matters and you'll want to untie later: any figure-eight beats the overhand equivalent.
