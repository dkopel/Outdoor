# Taut-Line Hitch

## What it does
The taut-line hitch is an adjustable loop knot — slide it along the standing line to tension or loosen a guyline without retying. It's the right knot for tent and tarp guylines, hammock ridgelines you want to tighten, and any rope you'll re-tension over the course of a night as conditions change.

## How to tie it
1. Pass the working end around a stake or anchor and back toward yourself.
2. Make two wraps around the standing line on the loop side (the side closer to the stake), working back away from the anchor.
3. Bring the working end around the standing line one more time, this time on the outside (the side away from the anchor), and tuck through.
4. Dress the knot so the wraps sit neatly; pull the standing line to seat it.
5. To adjust: hold the knot itself and slide it toward or away from the anchor.

## When to use it
Use the taut-line hitch any time you might need to tighten a line — tent guyouts, ridgelines, tarp corners in changing weather. It holds under tension but releases when you grip the knot and slide it. On slick modern cord (line-lock plastic cord, slippery Dyneema), it can creep; backup with a half-hitch or use a different adjustable system on those materials.

## Variations
The "midshipman's hitch" is a similar adjustable knot tied slightly differently; in practice, either works. Modern alternatives like a line-locker or a Prusik loop on a fixed line offer better grip on slick line. The taut-line is the right beginner choice when you have basic nylon or polypropylene cord.
