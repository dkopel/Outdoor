# Tornadoes and Severe Storms in the Outdoors

## Recognize the threat
Severe thunderstorms can produce tornadoes, large hail, straight-line winds of 60+ mph, and frequent lightning. Outdoor risk is high in the central US (Great Plains, Midwest, Southeast) from spring through summer, but tornadoes have happened in every state. Warning signs from the cloud base: rotation, a wall cloud (lowering hanging from the storm base), a green or unusual gray-purple sky, very large hail (golf ball or bigger), or an audible roar like a freight train. If a tornado warning is issued for your county, take immediate shelter.

## Best shelter outdoors
There is no truly safe outdoor option in a tornado. The least-bad: get to a low-lying area (ditch, ravine, drainage) below ground level, lie face down, cover your head with your arms, and stay there. Avoid tall isolated trees (lightning + blow-down), open fields (full debris exposure), under bridges or overpasses (wind acceleration through them is dangerous, not protective — old advice was wrong). If a building is nearby, even a small outbuilding is better than nothing. Vehicles are NOT safe in a tornado.

## Large hail
Golf-ball or larger hail can cause head injuries and break bones. Shelter under solid cover — your hardback pack on your shoulders helps; even a thick foam sleeping pad held over your head reduces impact. Get to your vehicle if close; hail dents cars but won't break heads inside. Crouch low under cover, away from windows and tent walls. Hail typically lasts 10–20 minutes; if you're protected for that window, the worst passes.

## Straight-line winds
Severe-thunderstorm winds can hit 80+ mph and topple trees. Get away from heavily-treed areas if a severe warning is imminent — campsites under big trees become deadly when limbs and trunks come down. Open ground with low brush is safer than under canopy. If caught in vehicle, pull off the road and away from anything that can fall on the car (trees, signs, power lines). Wait 20 minutes after the storm passes before driving — secondary cells follow.

## Get the warnings before they arrive
A NOAA Weather Radio receiver is small, runs on batteries, and broadcasts alerts for severe weather even when cell signal fails. Many satellite messengers also forward weather alerts. Before any spring/summer trip in tornado country, check the Storm Prediction Center outlook for the day — moderate or higher risks should change your plans. If the forecast says "afternoon and evening severe storms," your tent should be down and you should be off open ground by mid-afternoon.
