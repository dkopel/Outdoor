# Whiteout Conditions

A whiteout is loss of visibility caused by blowing snow, dense fog, or — most dangerously — a uniform overcast over a snowfield where snow and sky merge into a single featureless field of view. Whiteouts disorient even experienced people quickly, and disorientation in winter terrain kills.

## How whiteouts cause trouble
- **No horizon**: you cannot tell up from down, near from far. Slope steepness becomes invisible.
- **No depth perception**: a 6-inch step and a 10-foot cornice can look identical.
- **No landmarks**: you cannot navigate by sight.
- **Cold and wind**: whiteouts are usually accompanied by hypothermia-grade conditions.
- **Cornices and crevasses**: invisible drops become a serious fall hazard.

## If a whiteout starts to develop
The right move is almost always to stop traveling. Visibility deteriorates faster than it returns. If you have shelter or a known safe terrain feature within easy reach (your tent, a vehicle, a hut, a low-angle ridge in trees), get to it before vision drops further. If not, stop and shelter in place — dig a snow trench, set up a tent, or huddle behind a tarp wind break before the cold and confusion compound.

## If you must travel
Use a compass on a known bearing (GPS battery in a warm pocket); do not "navigate by sight." Rope the group together so no one becomes separated. Move slowly and probe the snow ahead with a pole. Travel in step-line: leader places each foot deliberately and the group follows, never spreading out. Set a turnaround time and stick to it.

## Don't navigate to "where I think I am"
The two most common whiteout mistakes are walking in circles (humans naturally drift in low-vis conditions without a compass) and walking off cornices or into hidden terrain. A magnetic compass that you trust over your eyes is the cure for both.

## Prevention
Whiteouts are forecastable. Check the mountain forecast and any local avalanche center's discussion before you leave. If conditions look uncertain, plan a turnaround time and a wind-protected camp option, and carry shelter capable of riding out 24 hours of bad weather.

Whiteouts are when "I'll just push on" gets people killed. Stopping early is the strong move.
