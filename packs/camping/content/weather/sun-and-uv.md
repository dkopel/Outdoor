# Sun and UV Exposure

UV exposure in the backcountry is more intense than most people expect. Altitude increases UV by roughly 4% per 1,000 feet of elevation. Snow reflects up to 80% of UV back at you; water and sand reflect 10–25%. A clear day above 8,000 feet with snow cover can deliver a serious burn in under an hour, even to people who rarely burn at sea level.

## Sunscreen
Apply a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen to all exposed skin 15–30 minutes before going outside. Reapply every two hours, and immediately after sweating heavily, swimming, or wiping your face. Don't forget commonly missed areas: ears, back of neck, underside of nose, top of feet (in sandals), and lips (use SPF lip balm).

## Clothing as the primary defense
Sun-protective clothing is more reliable than sunscreen because it doesn't wash off. A long-sleeve sun shirt (UPF 30+), wide-brim hat, and lightweight long pants protect more skin with less hassle. A neck gaiter or buff under your hat protects the back of the neck — a common burn spot from looking up at views.

## Eyes
UV damage to the eyes is cumulative and can cause "snow blindness" (photokeratitis) — painful, temporary, but disabling. Wear sunglasses with full UV protection (UV 400 or "100% UVA/UVB" labeled). At high altitude or on snow, use wraparound or glacier glasses with side shields. Cheap drugstore sunglasses without UV protection are worse than no glasses — they dilate the pupil while letting UV through.

## Recognizing sunburn early
A normal sunburn shows redness 2–6 hours after exposure and peaks at 12–24 hours. Severe burns may blister, cause chills, fever, nausea, or fatigue (sun poisoning). Cool the skin with damp cloth, hydrate, take ibuprofen or acetaminophen for pain, and avoid further sun until healed. Blistered burns covering large areas may require medical care.

## Snow blindness
Symptoms: eyes feel "gritty" or burning, painful in light, watery, vision blurry. Onset is usually 6–12 hours after exposure. Treatment: rest the eyes in darkness (close them, wear dark goggles or a blindfold) for 24–48 hours. Cool compresses help. Use lubricating drops if available. Do not rub. Most cases resolve on their own; severe cases need medical evaluation.

## Plan
Plan strenuous activity for morning when UV is lower. Use shade. Reapply sunscreen often. Sunburn is preventable.
