# Treating Burns in the Field

## Stop the burning
Move the person away from the heat source. If clothing is on fire and they can't drop and roll, smother flames with a non-synthetic blanket or jacket. If hot or melted material is stuck to the skin, do not pull it off — cut away cloth around it. For chemical burns, brush off dry chemical first, then flush with large amounts of clean water for at least 20 minutes.

## Cool the burn
Run cool (not ice-cold) water over the burned area for 10–20 minutes. If running water is not available, soak a clean cloth in cool water and lay it over the burn, replacing it every minute or so as it warms. Cooling is most effective in the first 20 minutes after injury but is still worth doing later. Do not apply ice, butter, toothpaste, or "burn ointment" of unknown origin.

## Cover and protect
After cooling, cover the burn loosely with sterile gauze or a clean, non-fluffy cloth. Plastic cling film (the kitchen type) is also acceptable as a sterile covering if available. Do not pop blisters. Do not wrap tightly — burned tissue swells, and tight wraps can cut off circulation. Remove jewelry from the burned area before swelling sets in.

## When to evacuate
Treat as serious and evacuate when: the burn is larger than the person's palm; it involves the face, hands, feet, genitals, or a joint; it is deep enough to look white, leathery, or charred; the person inhaled smoke or hot air; or it was caused by electricity or chemicals. All deep or large burns risk infection and fluid loss — get to definitive care as quickly as conditions allow.
