# Controlling External Bleeding

## Recognize life-threatening bleeding
Life-threatening bleeding is bleeding that is spurting, pooling on the ground, soaking through clothing, or that won't stop with simple pressure. If the person becomes pale, cold, sweaty, confused, or weak, treat as life-threatening regardless of how the wound looks. Get emergency help started (call, send a runner, or activate a PLB/satellite SOS) before or while you treat.

## Apply firm direct pressure
Press hard directly on the wound with a clean cloth, gauze, or your gloved hand. Use both hands and lean your body weight into it. Do not lift the pressure to "check" — once started, keep pressure unbroken for at least 10 minutes. If blood soaks through, add more material on top; do not remove what is already in place. Pressure is the single most effective field intervention for most wounds.

## When to use a tourniquet
A tourniquet is appropriate for life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that cannot be controlled with direct pressure, or in any situation where you cannot stay with the wound (you must move them, you are alone treating multiple casualties, the wound is inaccessible). Place it 2–3 inches above the wound (never on a joint), tighten until bleeding stops, and note the time. Do not loosen it once applied — that decision belongs to medical professionals.

## What to avoid
Do not use a thin string, cord, or boot lace as a tourniquet — these cut tissue without occluding arteries. Do not elevate or apply ice in place of pressure. Do not pour disinfectant into a deep wound. Do not assume that bleeding has "stopped" if the person becomes still and quiet — they may be going into shock. Keep pressure and keep them warm.
