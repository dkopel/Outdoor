# Lightning Strike Injuries

## What lightning does to a person
Lightning can injure through five mechanisms: direct strike (rare, often lethal), side flash (current jumps from a nearby struck object to a person), ground current (the most common — current spreads through the ground and across the body), contact (touching something while it's struck), and blunt trauma (thrown by the explosive air pressure or falling). The injuries that result: cardiac arrest is the most common cause of death; secondary causes include traumatic injuries, burns, neurological damage, ruptured eardrums.

## Lightning victims are safe to touch
A common myth: people who are struck retain dangerous electrical charge. They do not. Touch them immediately, start care without hesitation. They are not "live" and won't shock you.

## Priority: check breathing and pulse
Most lightning strike deaths occur from cardiac arrest in the seconds after the strike. CPR can be life-saving. With multiple victims, "reverse triage" applies: in a normal mass-casualty scenario, you prioritize victims showing signs of life over those who appear dead. For lightning, the opposite — victims who appear dead (no breathing, no pulse) often recover with prompt CPR; victims who are breathing and conscious typically survive on their own. Start CPR on the "apparently dead" first.

## CPR for lightning victims
Same technique as standard CPR: 30 compressions, 2 breaths, repeat. Continue until they breathe on their own, professional help takes over, you cannot continue, or the area becomes unsafe. Lightning victims can sometimes be revived after extended CPR (10–20+ minutes) because the immediate damage isn't the same as a heart attack — the heart can recover if oxygenation is maintained. Don't give up early.

## Other injuries
After the cardiac/respiratory priority:
- **Burns**: lightning burns are sometimes superficial fern-like patterns on skin (Lichtenberg figures, often faded by the time medical care arrives). Sometimes deeper at entry/exit points (head, feet, side of body).
- **Broken bones / spinal injuries**: from being thrown or falling.
- **Hearing loss / ruptured eardrums**: from the explosive shock wave.
- **Eye injuries / temporary blindness**: from the flash.
- **Confusion, amnesia, personality changes**: neurological damage that can be temporary or permanent.
- **Numbness / paralysis**: usually temporary; lightning paralysis can resolve over hours.

## Treat secondary injuries
After airway/breathing/circulation are managed: control bleeding, splint broken bones, cover burns with sterile dressings, treat for shock (cover, keep warm). Suspect spinal injury for any thrown victim and stabilize the head/neck.

## Evacuate every strike victim
Even lightning victims who feel fine after a strike need hospital evaluation. Cardiac complications can develop hours later. Internal burns may not be visible. Neurological damage may show up gradually. Activate emergency response, document time of strike and what you did, evacuate to medical care fast.

## Multiple victims
A close strike can injure several people simultaneously through ground current. Approach the scene only after assessing whether more strikes are likely (storm still nearby = wait; storm passed = approach). Triage:
1. Anyone unresponsive — start CPR immediately (reverse triage).
2. Anyone breathing but unconscious — recovery position, monitor.
3. Anyone conscious but injured — assess, treat trauma, prepare for evacuation.

Note all victims — even ones who say "I'm fine" — need evaluation.

## After the strike
The storm that produced the strike likely produces more. Don't stay at the strike location to render aid if conditions are still dangerous — move victims to safer ground (under treeline if above, away from open water if near) before working on them. If aerial evacuation is coming, mark the location clearly but don't stand in the open with a bright marker waving — that's exactly what attracts lightning.

## Prevention is the real answer
See weather.lightning-safety for the prevention strategy. If you can hear thunder, lightning is in strike range. Get out of exposed terrain. Assume lightning position if caught in open. Spread out from companions so one strike doesn't injure the whole group. The best lightning first aid is not needing it.
