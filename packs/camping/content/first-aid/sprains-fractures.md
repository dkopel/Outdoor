# Sprains and Suspected Fractures

## Telling sprains from fractures in the field
You often cannot tell the difference without an X-ray. Treat any injury with significant pain, swelling, deformity, or inability to bear weight as a possible fracture. Signs that strongly suggest a fracture: visible deformity, bone protruding, grinding sensation, point tenderness directly over bone, or the limb angled in an unnatural way. When in doubt, splint it.

## RICE for sprains
For straightforward ankle or wrist sprains without deformity: Rest (stop using the joint), Ice (or cold stream water — 15–20 minutes at a time, never directly on skin), Compression (wrap firmly but not so tight it cuts off circulation — toes/fingers should stay pink and warm), and Elevation (above heart level when possible). Avoid aggressive massage in the first day. Pain that doesn't improve over 24–48 hours or that worsens with weight-bearing needs medical evaluation.

## Splinting a suspected fracture
Splint the joint above and the joint below the suspected break to immobilize it. Use trekking poles, sleeping pad strips, sticks, or even a foam roll — whatever is rigid. Pad bony spots with cloth so the splint doesn't cause its own pressure injury. Secure with cloth strips, tape, or cord — snug but not constricting. Recheck circulation, sensation, and movement in the fingers/toes every 15–20 minutes. If the limb is angled badly and circulation is impaired (pale, cold, no pulse), apply gentle in-line traction to restore alignment, then splint.

## Open fractures and dislocations
An open fracture (bone has broken through skin) is a high-infection-risk emergency. Cover the wound with sterile dressing, do not push bone back in, splint, and evacuate fast. For obvious dislocations of major joints (shoulder, hip, knee), do not attempt to reduce them in the field unless you have training and the person's circulation is compromised — splint as-is and evacuate. Finger dislocations are sometimes reduced by trained outdoor first responders; lay people should splint and seek care.
