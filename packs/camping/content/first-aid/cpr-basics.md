# CPR Basics — Reference Only

## This is not training
This page describes the steps of bystander CPR as a memory aid for someone who has been trained. Formal CPR training (4 hours, in person, with a manikin) is essential before you'll perform it effectively under stress. Take a Red Cross or AHA class before relying on this material. Carry a pocket CPR mask if you can.

## Check, call, compress
Check responsiveness — tap the shoulder and shout. If unresponsive, send someone for help or activate a PLB/satellite SOS yourself. Open the airway with a head tilt / chin lift and check for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Agonal gasping (irregular, gulping breaths) is not normal breathing — start CPR. If no normal breathing, begin chest compressions immediately.

## Compressions
Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), other hand on top. Lock your elbows and use your upper body weight to push hard and fast: at least 2 inches deep at a rate of 100–120 compressions per minute (the tempo of "Stayin' Alive"). Let the chest fully recoil between compressions — do not lean on the chest. Minimize interruptions; every pause hurts perfusion. Hands-only CPR (continuous compressions, no rescue breaths) is the recommended approach for untrained or under-equipped bystanders for adult cardiac arrest.

## When to stop
Continue until: trained help takes over, you are physically unable to continue, the area becomes unsafe, or you see clear signs of life (the person moves, makes sounds, or starts breathing normally). In wilderness settings with no realistic chance of advanced care arriving, the decision to stop is yours — there is no rule. Document the time you stopped if possible.
