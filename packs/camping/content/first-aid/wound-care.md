# Wound Care and Blisters

## Cleaning a minor wound
Once bleeding is controlled, the most important thing for non-life-threatening wounds is thorough cleaning. Flush with clean drinkable water under pressure — a squeeze of water from a bag punctured with a pinhole, or a syringe if you carry one, works well. Aim for at least 500 ml of flushing for a small wound, more for one with visible debris. Remove embedded gravel or splinters with clean tweezers if accessible. Mild soap on the surrounding skin is fine; harsh disinfectants (hydrogen peroxide, undiluted iodine) inside the wound damage tissue and slow healing.

## Dressing the wound
After cleaning, apply a thin layer of antibiotic ointment (if you carry it) and cover with a sterile non-stick pad held by tape or a bandage. Wrap firmly enough to stay in place but never so tight that it cuts off circulation. Change the dressing daily, or sooner if it gets wet, dirty, or saturated. Watch for spreading redness, increasing warmth, pus, or red streaks running away from the wound — these are signs of infection and warrant evacuation.

## Blister prevention
Blisters form where skin is rubbed repeatedly. Prevent them by wearing well-fitted shoes broken in before the trip, moisture-wicking socks, and stopping at the first hint of a hot spot to address it. The moment you feel friction, stop and cover the spot with athletic tape, moleskin, or a blister patch. Do not "push through" — a small hot spot fixed in 60 seconds beats hours hobbling on a popped blister.

## Treating blisters
If a blister is intact and small, leave it alone — the fluid-filled skin is the best dressing. Pad around it with a doughnut of moleskin to take pressure off. If the blister is large, painful, or going to be rubbed regardless, drain it: clean the surface, sterilize a needle in flame, prick the edge near skin level, press out fluid gently, leave the skin in place, and cover with antibiotic ointment and a non-stick dressing. Do not peel off the skin — it protects raw tissue underneath while it heals.
