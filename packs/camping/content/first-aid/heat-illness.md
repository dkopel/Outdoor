# Heat Exhaustion and Heat Stroke

## Heat exhaustion vs heat stroke
Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, cool/clammy skin, weakness, nausea, headache, fast weak pulse, normal or mildly elevated core temperature. The person is conscious and usually still sweating. Heat stroke is a medical emergency: core temperature above ~104°F, skin can be hot and dry OR still sweating, confusion or unconsciousness, rapid pulse, sometimes seizures. Altered mental state in heat = heat stroke until proven otherwise.

## Treat heat exhaustion
Move to shade. Loosen or remove excess clothing. Cool with wet cloths or fanning, especially on the neck, armpits, and groin. Have the person sip cool water or an electrolyte drink slowly — too much too fast triggers vomiting. Rest in a cool spot for at least 30 minutes. If they don't improve in that window, or get worse, treat as heat stroke.

## Treat heat stroke
Cool the person aggressively, fast. If you can, immerse the torso in cool water (stream, lake, full bottles poured over them). Otherwise wet their entire body with whatever water you have and fan vigorously to drive evaporation. Pack ice or cold-water bottles at neck, armpits, and groin. Stop active cooling when they become alert or shiver. Do not force liquids on an unconscious or confused person. Evacuate immediately — even if they recover, heat stroke causes organ damage that needs hospital care.

## Prevention
Drink to thirst plus a little more in hot conditions — roughly 1 liter per 1–2 hours of moderate exertion in the heat, but adjust to your sweat rate. Eat salty food to replace electrolytes; plain water alone is not enough on long hot days. Schedule hard effort for cooler hours. Wear loose, light-colored, breathable clothing and a wide-brim hat. Recognize early warning signs (headache, irritability, dizziness) and stop before things escalate.
