# Head Injury and Concussion in the Backcountry

Any blow to the head, fall onto the head, or sudden whiplash-type force can cause a concussion or worse traumatic brain injury (TBI). Symptoms can be obvious immediately or take hours to days to appear. Treat all head trauma seriously, even when the person says they "feel fine" right after.

## Signs of a concussion
**Physical**: headache, dizziness, nausea or vomiting, vision changes, light or noise sensitivity, balance problems, fatigue
**Cognitive**: confusion, memory problems, slowed thinking, difficulty concentrating, feeling "foggy"
**Emotional**: irritability, sadness, anxiety, increased emotionality
**Sleep**: trouble falling asleep, sleeping too much or too little

A person can have a concussion without losing consciousness. Brief confusion right after a hit ("What happened?", repeated questions) is one of the most common signs.

## Emergency warning signs — evacuate immediately
The CDC lists these as 911-level danger signs in adults. In the backcountry they mean activate emergency rescue (PLB, satellite communicator, or send a runner for help) and start evacuation:
- Loss of consciousness for any period
- A headache that gets steadily worse rather than better
- Repeated vomiting
- Slurred speech
- One pupil larger than the other
- Seizures or convulsions
- Increasing confusion, restlessness, or agitation
- Extreme drowsiness or cannot be woken up
- Weakness, numbness, or loss of coordination
- Clear fluid or blood draining from the ears or nose

Any one of these signals possible brain bleeding or severe TBI — get to a hospital immediately.

## What to do in the field
1. Stop activity. Do not let the person continue hiking, climbing, scrambling, or anything else requiring balance or judgment.
2. Sit or lie them down in a safe spot. Protect from cold and rain.
3. Check the rest of the body for other injuries — falls causing head trauma usually injure other parts too.
4. Watch them continuously. Symptoms can develop or worsen over hours.
5. Wake them every 1–2 hours through the first night if they are alone. If they cannot be woken normally, treat as an emergency.
6. For pain in the first 24 hours, use acetaminophen (Tylenol) only. **Avoid ibuprofen, aspirin, and naproxen** — these reduce blood clotting and could worsen a brain bleed.
7. No alcohol. No physical exertion. Rest.
8. Evacuate to medical evaluation as soon as the terrain allows, even if the person seems okay. A normal-seeming person with a recent head injury can deteriorate without warning.

## Recovery
Most concussions improve in 1–2 weeks with rest. Symptoms that persist beyond that, or that improve and then suddenly worsen, need medical evaluation. Do not return to strenuous outdoor activity until symptom-free and cleared by a physician.

This is general first aid guidance. Always seek professional medical evaluation after any head injury.
