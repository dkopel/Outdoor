# Blisters

A blister is a small injury but a trip-ender — and a serious blister that opens and gets infected in the field is a real medical problem. The right move is to prevent them, catch them early, and never let a "hot spot" become a blister.

## Prevent
- **Break in new boots** at home: short walks, longer walks, day hikes — before any multi-day trip.
- **Wear two socks**: a thin liner sock under a thicker outer sock. The friction happens between the two socks, not between sock and skin.
- **Use synthetic or wool socks**, not cotton. Cotton holds sweat against the skin and accelerates blistering.
- **Keep feet dry**. Change socks midday if your feet are sweating heavily, especially in wet conditions. Dry feet at lunch. Some hikers carry a spare dry pair just for camp.
- **Tight enough, not too tight.** Boots that are too loose let your foot slide and rub. Too tight and you get pressure blisters and cold toes.
- **Lace correctly.** A "heel lock" or "surgeon's knot" at the ankle keeps the heel from lifting and rubbing on descents.

## Hot spots — act immediately
A "hot spot" is the warm, red, slightly tender skin that comes before a blister forms. If you feel one, stop right now. Do not try to push through to the next break. Sit down, pull off the boot, and:
- Clean and dry the skin.
- Apply moleskin or a similar friction-reducing patch, cut to cover the area plus a half-inch on every side. Or apply Leukotape (a stiff sports tape) directly to the skin — many hikers consider this the most reliable option.
- Check the fit and lacing.
- Continue. Hot spots caught this early do not become blisters.

## A formed blister
If a blister has already formed:
- **Small, unbroken**: protect with a doughnut-shaped piece of moleskin (cut a hole in the center) so the blister sits in the hole, not under pressure. Cover with tape. Do not drain it — intact skin is the best protection against infection.
- **Large or under pressure**: drain it carefully. Wash hands and the blister with soap and water or alcohol wipes. Sterilize a needle with flame or alcohol. Pierce the blister at its lowest edge in two spots. Press gently to express fluid. Leave the skin in place as a natural bandage. Apply antibiotic ointment, gauze, and tape.
- **Broken or torn**: clean thoroughly with sterile water or saline. Apply antibiotic ointment, non-stick gauze, and tape. Watch for infection over the next 48 hours.

## Signs of infection — seek medical care
Spreading redness, increasing pain, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks running up the limb, or fever. These need antibiotics and evaluation, not more hiking.

Foot care is morale management. A small first aid kit with moleskin, Leukotape, alcohol wipes, a few gauze pads, and a needle is the difference between a hard day and a ruined trip.
