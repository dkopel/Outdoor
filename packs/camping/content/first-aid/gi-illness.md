# Backcountry Diarrhea and Vomiting

## Why this matters
A camper with bad diarrhea and vomiting can be dangerously dehydrated within 12 hours, especially in heat or at altitude. GI illness also spreads fast through a group sharing a kitchen — one infected hand on a shared spoon takes out a whole trip. Common causes: norovirus (transmits person-to-person), giardia or cryptosporidium from untreated water, foodborne bacteria, viral stomach bugs picked up before the trip.

## Field treatment for the patient
The single priority is rehydration. Have them sip — not gulp — small frequent amounts of water, ideally with a pinch of salt and a small spoon of sugar per liter (oral rehydration). Commercial electrolyte powders are designed for exactly this and pack well. If they're vomiting, wait 10–15 minutes after a vomit and try a teaspoon of fluid every few minutes; slowly increase. Avoid milk, alcohol, caffeine, fatty foods, and large meals for 24 hours. Bland starches (crackers, rice, pasta) help once the stomach settles.

## When to evacuate
Evacuate when: signs of severe dehydration (no urination for 8+ hours, very dark urine, confusion, fainting), blood or pus in the stool, fever above 102°F, severe abdominal pain that's worsening, vomiting that won't stop after 12+ hours, the patient cannot keep any fluids down, or symptoms last more than 3 days without clear improvement. In a frail person, child, or older adult, evacuate sooner. Severe ongoing fluid loss in the field is a medical emergency.

## Stopping the spread
Hand-washing with soap is the single biggest protection — before cooking, before eating, after the bathroom. Hand sanitizer (60%+ alcohol) is OK as a backup but doesn't kill norovirus or some parasites well; soap and water is better. Designate one person to handle communal food once illness appears in the group and have them wash even more rigorously. Sick person uses dedicated dishes/utensils. Bury feces 200 ft from water and camp, in a 6–8 inch cathole, or pack out with a wag bag. Toilet paper should be packed out.

## Prevention
Treat all backcountry water (see water domain). Wash hands with soap before every meal, period. Don't share water bottles or spoons. Pre-trip: don't start a trip while recovering from gastro — give the bug 48 hours to clear before exposing the rest of the group. Pack a small first-aid kit with a basic electrolyte powder and an over-the-counter anti-diarrheal that you've used before; do not start one for the first time mid-trip.
