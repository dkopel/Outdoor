# Fire Safety

## Before you light
Check current fire restrictions for the area you're in. Many public lands have seasonal stage-1, stage-2, or full bans — burning during a ban is illegal and can spread a wildfire that kills people and burns hundreds of square miles. If there's an existing fire ring or established pit, use it; don't build a new one. Clear a 10-foot radius around the fire site of all flammable debris (leaves, pine needles, twigs) down to bare mineral soil. Have a water container or sand piled within arm's reach before you strike a match.

## While it burns
Never leave a fire unattended, not even for a minute to go grab water from the stream. Keep flames knee-high or smaller; an established fire is about the size you can warm your hands at, not a bonfire. Don't burn trash — most modern packaging produces toxic smoke and aluminum foil that won't fully burn. Keep loose hair, scarves, jacket cords, and gear away from the flame and the rising heat. Place fuel by hand; never throw it.

## Extinguishing
A fire is out only when the ashes are cold to the touch. Pour water on the fire, stir the ashes with a stick, pour again, and feel the entire area with your bare hand. If it's warm anywhere, repeat. Coals can stay alive under wet ash for days and reignite when the wind picks up. Walk the perimeter, looking for smoldering material in nearby duff. In dry conditions, drowning a fire takes more water than you think — sometimes gallons.

## Leave no trace
Pack out everything that doesn't burn: foil, glass, twist ties, food packaging. Scatter the cold ashes widely after they're fully out. Dismantle any fire ring you built so the site looks unused. The best outdoor ethic is "leave it better than you found it" — pack out any fire-ring trash a previous user left. Wildfire prevention is a personal responsibility; one careless campfire can rewrite a landscape for fifty years.
