# Building a Fire

## The three sizes of fuel
A fire needs three classes of material, in increasing size. Tinder: anything that takes a spark or flame and burns easily — dry grass, birch bark, fine wood shavings, pine pitch wood (fatwood), commercial tinder. Kindling: pencil-thin to thumb-thin dead twigs broken from standing dead branches (not picked off the ground, which is wet). Fuel: progressively larger split or broken pieces, wrist-thick and up. Gather all three sizes before you light a single match — fumbling in the dark for more kindling is how fires die.

## The teepee lay
Stack tinder loosely on a base of small sticks so it doesn't sit in cold dirt or wet ground. Build a teepee of kindling around the tinder, leaving an opening on the windward side to feed in flame and oxygen. Light the tinder through the opening. As the kindling catches, add slightly larger pieces, always preserving the teepee shape — heat rises, so concentrated kindling at the top builds fast. The teepee is the easiest lay for starting; it's not the most efficient for cooking or long burns.

## The log cabin (for sustained burn)
Lay two thumb-thick sticks parallel, a few inches apart, then two more across them perpendicular, building up like Lincoln Logs. Place tinder and small kindling in the middle. Light the tinder. As the structure burns, the rising heat lights the upper logs and the whole stack falls into a clean coal bed — excellent for cooking. The "upside-down" or "top-down" variant (largest at the bottom, smallest on top, tinder at the top) is even better for unattended burns; it produces less smoke and a steady cooking heat.

## Lighting and feeding
Match flame goes upward, so light at the bottom of the tinder. Cup your hands around the new flame to shield it from wind. Blow gently and steadily — gusts blow flames out. Add fuel small first, larger as the fire develops; throwing wrist-thick fuel onto a baby fire smothers it. A fire that is just starting needs more oxygen than wood; once established, more wood than oxygen. Read the fire and respond.
