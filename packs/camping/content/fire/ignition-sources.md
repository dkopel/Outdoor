# Ignition Sources

## Carry redundancy
The ten essentials include "fire" because the consequences of no fire in an unplanned overnight can be lethal. Carry at least two independent ignition sources — and "two lighters" doesn't count, because they both fail in cold or wet the same way. Practical combination: a lighter + waterproof matches in a sealed container + a ferro rod with steel striker. Each works when the others don't.

## Lighters
A standard Bic-style lighter is light, cheap, reliable in dry conditions, and produces flame for minutes per fill. Failures: wet butane doesn't light, cold (below ~40°F) butane doesn't vaporize, the wheel-and-flint mechanism freezes. Strategies: keep one in an inside pocket near body heat; carry a piezo-electric lighter as a second choice (no flint to fail in cold). Always test fire at the start of each trip; a lighter left in a glove box all winter may not work.

## Waterproof matches
Stormproof matches (UCO, REI brand) burn even when wet and in wind; standard "waterproof" matches just have a coating that resists initial water exposure. Store in a sealed waterproof container (small Nalgene-style, or a 35mm film canister, or commercial match safes). Include a striker strip taped inside the container — matchbox strikers wear out and a separate one is insurance. Practice lighting one in the rain at home before relying on it.

## Ferro rods (fire steels)
A ferrocerium rod scraped with a steel striker produces 3,000°F+ sparks that ignite tinder. Advantages: works wet, works cold, lasts thousands of strikes per rod, no fuel to run out. Disadvantage: needs good tinder and practice. The skill is in catching a spark on tinder you can blow into flame. Best tinder for ferro rods: char cloth, fatwood shavings, cotton ball with petroleum jelly, dry birch bark fibers, dryer lint, commercial fire-starter cubes. Practice at home until you can reliably get flame.

## Backup tinder
Carry pre-made tinder in addition to harvest-able tinder. A few cotton balls saturated with petroleum jelly in a small zip bag weigh almost nothing and ignite from anything. Commercial fire-starter cubes (UCO Sweetfire, Esbit) burn for 10 minutes in wind and rain. Fatwood sticks (pine pitch-saturated heartwood) shave into curls that ignite easily and burn hot. Many hikers carry one or two of these as redundancy with their ignition sources.

## Lighting in worst conditions
Standing in cold rain trying to light a fire is when the system gets tested. Get out of wind under any cover available. Stack dry tinder + kindling + small fuel before striking the first match. Use commercial tinder if natural is wet. Cup hands around the flame. Don't put a small flame down on cold wet ground; it dies. If after 5 minutes you don't have ignition, stop, get under shelter, and reassess — wet conditions plus cold plus failing ignition can spiral into hypothermia. Sometimes the right call is to skip the fire and rely on shelter and insulation.
