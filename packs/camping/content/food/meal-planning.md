# Meal Planning

## Calorie targets by trip type
Day hikes: 2,500–3,500 calories total (snacks + lunch). Standard backpacking (10–15 mile days, 2,000–3,000 ft elevation): 3,000–4,000 calories per day. Heavy mountaineering days (long ascents, cold weather): 4,500–6,000 calories. Winter expeditions: 5,000+ calories. Most beginners pack too little food — assume you're underestimating and add 10%. Children and teens eat at adult-or-higher levels on big days.

## Weight per day targets
General rule: 1.5–2.0 pounds (700–900 g) of food per person per day. Below 1.5 lb risks under-fueling. Above 2 lb adds weight you'll wish you didn't have. Achieve targets through energy-dense foods (high fat = 9 cal/g vs carbs/protein 4 cal/g). Repackage commercial food into zip bags — save the weight of cardboard boxes and excess plastic. A small kitchen scale at home is useful for dialing in trip food weight before you carry it.

## Meal structure
**Breakfast** (400–700 cal): instant oats with milk powder + nuts + dried fruit + sugar; granola with milk powder; backpacker breakfast skillets; cold-soaked overnight oats. Quick to make, gets you moving. **Trail food** (1,000–2,000 cal across the day): bars, trail mix, dried meat, cheese (cool weather), nut butters, fig newtons, candy. Eat continuously while walking. **Dinner** (700–1,200 cal): freeze-dried meal, or boil-add-water foods like instant mashed potatoes, ramen, couscous, mac & cheese. Hot meal does morale work beyond just calories.

## Dehydrated and freeze-dried
Commercial freeze-dried meals (Mountain House, Backpacker's Pantry, etc.) are convenient and reasonably light but expensive and salty. Home dehydration with a $50 dehydrator is much cheaper and lets you make meals you actually like. Couscous + olive oil + spices + freeze-dried vegetables is a 700-calorie dinner you can assemble at home in 5 minutes. Cold-soaked meals (instant mashed potatoes, ramen) save fuel and are popular with ultralight thru-hikers.

## Trail snacks
The best trail snacks: dense, calorie-rich, durable, no prep. Examples: nuts (220 cal/oz roasted; peanuts, almonds, walnuts), peanut butter packets (190 cal each), candy bars (240–300 cal), trail mix with chocolate (130 cal/oz), dried fruit (90 cal/oz; figs, dates, mango, raisins), hard cheese (cool weather, 110 cal/oz), beef jerky (80 cal/oz), tortillas (200 cal each, pack flat). Pre-portion snacks into bags so you don't eat the next day's by accident.

## Packing groceries vs special foods
For weekend trips, regular grocery food repackaged is fine and cheap — tortillas, peanut butter, instant noodles, hard cheese, salami, bagels, etc. For week-plus trips, lighter freeze-dried becomes worth it because the weight savings add up. Long thru-hikes use resupply boxes mailed to addresses on trail. Always carry one extra day of food for an unplanned weather delay or slow day. Extra food at the end is fine; running short on day 4 is a problem.
