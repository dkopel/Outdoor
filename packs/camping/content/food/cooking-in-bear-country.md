# Cooking and Eating in Bear Country

Where you cook and eat matters as much as how you store food. Smells travel farther than most people realize, and a bear attracted by dinner smells does not distinguish between the kitchen and your sleeping bag at 2 AM.

## The bear triangle
Set up three points at least 100 feet (about 30 m) apart, ideally forming a triangle:
- **Sleeping area** (your tent)
- **Cooking and eating area** ("the kitchen")
- **Food storage** (canister, hang, or bear box)

In areas with very high bear pressure (Grand Teton, Yellowstone, Denali, parts of Alaska), the recommended distance is 200 feet. Check local regulations. The orientation matters too: place the kitchen and food storage **downwind** from your tent so the scent does not blow over your sleeping spot.

## Cook efficiently
- **Plan meals that cook fast** and produce minimal smelly cleanup. Freeze-dried meals (boil water, eat directly from the bag) leave the least mess and odor.
- **Minimize splatter and spillage.** Wipe your stove and pot immediately if anything spills.
- **No food in the tent. Ever.** Not snacks, not breakfast bars, not "just for tonight."

## Cleanup is part of cooking
- **Eat everything you cook.** Plan portion sizes. Pack out any food you cannot finish (even small scraps).
- **Strain dishwater** through a fine mesh or bandana to catch food bits. Pack the solids out with your trash.
- **Scatter the strained gray water widely**, 200 feet from camp and water sources — never pour it concentrated in one spot.
- **Wash hands** before bed.

## What is "food" — broader than you think
For storage and kitchen-triangle purposes, treat all of these as food:
- All edibles and drinks (including powdered drink mixes, hot chocolate, coffee, electrolyte tabs)
- **Toiletries**: toothpaste, lip balm, sunscreen, insect repellent, deodorant, soap, scented wipes, hand lotion
- **Trash and packaging**, even empty wrappers — residual scent is plenty
- **Stove fuel and the stove itself**
- **Pet food and dog treats**
- **Cookware that touched food**
- **Clothes that have absorbed food smells** — cooking apron, bandana you wiped your hands on

Everything above gets stored where the food goes. Sleep in clothes that have not been near the kitchen.

## A note on coolers and vehicles
In car-camping, coolers are **not** bear-resistant. A black bear can pop a cooler open in seconds and routinely peels back car windows for food smells. Use the campground's bear locker, no exceptions. A car with food in it parked at a trailhead is a known bear-magnet in places like the Sierra, Smokies, and Yosemite.

## If a bear comes into your kitchen
Stop cooking. Group up. Make yourself big and loud. Slowly back away to give the bear the kitchen and try to encourage it to leave by yelling and banging pots. Do not approach. Do not try to grab your food bag back from a bear. Once the bear is gone, take inventory of what you've lost and decide whether to move camp. Report the incident to rangers.

Proper kitchen practice protects you, protects future hikers, and protects the bear. A fed bear is, eventually, a dead bear.
