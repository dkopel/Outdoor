# Backcountry Food Planning

## Calories per day
A moderately active adult on trail typically burns 3,000–4,500 calories per day, more on heavy ascending days or in cold conditions. Pack at least 1.5 lb (700 g) of food per person per day for moderate trips, and 2 lb (900 g) per day for cold-weather or high-effort trips. Multi-day calorie deficit makes you slow, cold, and irritable — and risks cumulative hypothermia in cool conditions. Err on the side of more, especially for groups including teenagers or for trips longer than 4 days.

## Macronutrient mix
Fat is the most calorie-dense macronutrient (9 cal/g vs 4 for carbs and protein) and pack-friendly: olive oil, nut butter, hard cheese, summer sausage. Carbohydrates deliver fast-access energy and digest quickly — bars, dried fruit, candy. Protein supports muscle repair on multi-day trips and produces some metabolic heat overnight. A practical aim: about 50% calories from fat, 35% carbs, 15% protein for cool-weather or long trips; shift toward more carbs and less fat for short hot trips where fat slows digestion.

## What packs well
Foods that travel well: hard cheese (in cool weather), summer sausage, dehydrated meals, instant oats, peanut butter (single-serve packets or repackaged), nuts, dried fruit, jerky, tortillas (more durable than bread), chocolate, hard candy, energy bars, drink mixes. Avoid anything that requires refrigeration on long trips and anything fragile that becomes crumbs in a pack. Repackage from bulky retail packaging into zip bags to save weight and volume.

## Eat early, eat often
Breakfast and a steady stream of snacks fuel a day far better than a single big mid-day meal. Cold-weather camping especially benefits from a high-fat dinner consumed before getting in the sleeping bag — digestion produces internal heat. Drink water alongside calories; dehydration suppresses appetite and amplifies fatigue. Pre-pack snacks accessible without taking the pack off, so you actually eat them.
