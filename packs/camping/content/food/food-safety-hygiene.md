# Food Safety and Hygiene

## Hand washing
The single biggest preventer of group GI illness on a trip. Wash with soap and clean water (warmed in a pot if cold conditions) before every meal, before food prep, and after every bathroom break. Dishpan with a small amount of biodegradable soap, used 200 feet from water sources. Hand sanitizer (60%+ alcohol) is a backup for handwashing but doesn't kill norovirus or some parasites — soap and water always wins. One sick person can transmit a stomach bug to the whole group through unwashed hands at a shared snack bag.

## Communal vs personal food
Reduce shared-spoon risk: each person has their own utensil that doesn't touch communal food. Pour shared snacks into individual hands or cups rather than sticking hands into bags. Use a serving spoon for shared pots, not personal spoons. If anyone in the group is sick, switch them to dedicated dishes and have them stay out of cooking duty until 24 hours symptom-free.

## Cooking temperatures
Most backpacking food (dehydrated meals, instant oats, ramen) just needs boiling water — that's a kill temperature for bacteria. Fresh meat in cooler weather (first night meals) needs cooking to actual safe internal temperatures: poultry 165°F, ground meat 160°F, whole cuts 145°F + 3 minutes rest, fish 145°F. A small folding meat thermometer weighs almost nothing. Cooked food cooling overnight is risky — eat or refrigerate within 2 hours; in heat, 1 hour.

## Storing perishables
Coolers with ice last 1–3 days depending on conditions. First-night fresh food (steak, salmon, eggs) keeps best in the bottom of the cooler against frozen items. Pre-frozen meals act as both food and ice. Vacuum-sealed meat lasts longer than open-packaging. Past day 1–2, switch to non-perishable dehydrated/freeze-dried meals. In summer, even sealed cheese gets sweaty and risky after 36+ hours without cold storage.

## Dish washing
Use minimum water and biodegradable soap. Pre-scrape food residue into a trash bag (not into the dishwater). Wash in one pot, rinse in another, dry on a clean cloth. Scatter strained gray water 200 feet from water sources. Don't wash dishes in lakes or streams; soap residues are problems even biodegradable ones. Pack out food scraps, even small ones — they attract animals and don't decompose in cold or alpine environments.

## Common food illness triggers
Underprepared or undertreated water (see water.water-borne-illness). Cross-contamination from raw meat to clean food (use separate cutting boards or pre-prep at home). Sick person handling food. Sharing of utensils with someone sick. Cooled-then-warmed food sitting in the danger zone (40–140°F). Foods past their pull date taken on a trip because they were free. Trust your nose and eyes: anything that smells off or looks off, throw out.
