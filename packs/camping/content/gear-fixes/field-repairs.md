# Common Field Repairs

## Tent and tarp tears
Small tears: clean and dry the area, then apply Tenacious Tape or repair tape on both sides of the fabric, smoothing from the center outward. Duct tape works in a pinch but leaves residue and degrades in UV. For mesh, a small patch of Tenacious Mesh, or stitch closed with dental floss and a sail-repair needle. A long tear near a seam: stitch with floss in a baseball stitch, then cover both sides with tape to seal.

## Broken tent pole
Carry a pole repair sleeve (most tents include one). When a pole snaps, slide the sleeve over the break, center it, and crimp the ends with a Leatherman so it can't move. No sleeve? A 6-inch piece of straight stick taped along the break with duct tape and lashed with cord will get you through the night. Replace before next trip.

## Boot blowout
A sole separating from a boot can be re-glued with cyanoacrylate (super glue) if dry; press together and weight for 20 minutes. For a major separation, lash the sole to the upper with a wide strip of duct tape or cord wrapped around the boot's instep. A blown-out toe seam: stitch with dental floss using a heavy needle, then duct tape over the stitching. Field repairs are temporary — limp out, replace the boot.

## Stove won't light
Most canister stove issues are clogged jets. Disassemble per the stove's instructions and clean the jet with the wire on most stove tool sets (or a clean fine wire). For liquid-fuel stoves, the fuel line and pump cup are the most common failure points; carry a maintenance kit and learn to use it before a trip. A failed stove can ruin a cold trip — carry the maintenance kit and a backup ignition source (matches and a lighter, not just one).

## What to carry
A practical repair kit weighs under 8 oz and fixes most field problems: 6 feet of duct tape wrapped around a trekking pole or water bottle, Tenacious Tape, super glue, safety pins, a sail-repair needle and 20 feet of dental floss, 50 feet of nylon cord, a stove-specific maintenance kit if you carry a liquid stove, and a multi-tool with pliers. Practice the repairs at home before you need them in the dark in the rain.
