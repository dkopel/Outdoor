# A Practical Backcountry Repair Kit

The right small repair kit fits in a sandwich-sized bag and lets you fix 90% of common gear failures. Build one once, refresh it after every trip.

## Tape — the foundation
- **Duct tape**: 6–10 feet wrapped around a trekking pole, water bottle, or pencil stub. Fixes everything from torn tents to busted boot soles to taped-on splints. Wrap it tight so it doesn't peel.
- **Tenacious Tape** (clear adhesive nylon repair patches): for stuff sacks, sleeping bags, rain jackets, and tents. Cleaner repair than duct tape on technical fabrics; survives washing.
- **Leukotape or athletic tape**: for skin / blister repairs, can also fix gear in a pinch.

## Sewing
- A small **needle** with the eye threaded with about 2 feet of strong thread (dental floss works as thread and is dual-purpose). Useful for tear repair, button replacement, sewing a tent seam.
- **Safety pins** (3–4): emergency closures, zipper pulls, splinter removal.

## Cordage
- **2–3 feet of strong cord** (paracord or accessory cord): replacement laces, guy lines, repair lashings.
- **2–3 feet of bank line or strong thread**: stitching loads.

## Adhesives
- A small tube of **seam sealer** (for tents and rain gear) — or a packet of urethane glue.
- A few **alcohol prep pads**: clean surfaces before adhesion (and useful for first aid).

## Specific spare parts
- **Tent pole splint** (a 4-inch aluminum sleeve that slides over a broken pole). Many tents ship with one — pack it.
- **Spare buckles** or **buckle repair clips** matching your pack.
- **A spare lighter** in a dry bag.
- **Spare batteries** for your headlamp.

## Multitool or knife
A small knife and a pair of pliers covers most field repairs. A multitool with pliers, scissors, file, and small flat/Phillips screwdriver bits handles more. Don't bring something heavier than you'll actually use.

## What you don't need
- Specialty tools for specific gear unless you have a known weakness in that piece (e.g., a stove with a particular failure mode).
- A full sewing kit. The minimal items above are enough for any field repair.

## Where to keep it
Pack it where you can find it without unpacking your bag — top lid, hip pocket, or a labeled stuff sack near the top. The one time you need it, you will need it fast and in poor conditions.

## Pre-trip check
Inspect your gear before every trip. Most gear failures had warning signs the previous trip — a stitched seam pulling apart, a tent pole getting hard to bend, a worn buckle. Replace or repair at home where parts are cheap and time is plentiful.
