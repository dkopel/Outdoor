# Setting Up a Tent

## Site preparation
Clear the spot of sharp sticks, pine cones, and rocks — anything larger than a marble will be a stone in your back after a few hours. Look up: dead branches above you can fall in wind. Look down for slope: pitching with your head slightly uphill is far more comfortable than the reverse, and even a 5° slope lets cold air pool around your feet. Stay 200 feet (60 meters) from water sources where regulations require — it's both leave-no-trace and avoids surprise high water in storms.

## Pitching
Stake out the floor first, getting the corners taut and the floor flat with no wrinkles. Then erect poles and clip the body to them — most modern tents have color-coded poles or clips. Finally, attach and tension the rainfly. The fly should not touch the inner tent body anywhere — that's where rain wicks through. Guy out the fly attachment points so the fabric stands away from the body: most tents perform poorly until guylines are deployed and tensioned.

## Storm-pitching
If wind is up, orient the lowest, smallest end of the tent into the wind — usually the foot end. Use every guyline the tent has, double up stakes for the most stressed corners, and tension all guys equally so the fabric doesn't develop weak points. Avoid pitching in saddles between hills (wind channels), the crests of ridges (full exposure), or under big trees that may shed branches. In sustained high winds, valleys and timber are friends.

## Breaking camp without damage
Shake out the fly before stuffing — wet fabric in a wet stuff sack grows mildew fast. Disassemble poles by pushing from the center rather than pulling at the ends, which stresses internal shock cord. Wipe stakes clean. Pull stakes by lifting straight out, never sideways. If the tent is wet, pitch it later to dry rather than packing wet for more than 24 hours.
