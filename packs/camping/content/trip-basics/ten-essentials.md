# The Ten Essentials

## What and why
The "Ten Essentials" is a packing framework, not a fixed list — the idea is to carry tools for the most likely problems: navigation, weather, light, injury, fire, food, water, repair, and shelter. The point is being able to handle a forced unplanned overnight stay safely. Even for a "short day hike," carry the essentials in a small pack; people who turn day hikes into unintended overnight survival situations have started this way for as long as people have hiked.

## The list (modernized)
1. **Navigation** — map and compass at minimum; GPS or phone with offline maps as supplements. Know how to use them.
2. **Sun protection** — sunscreen, sunglasses, brimmed hat, lip balm with UV block. Sunburn and snow blindness ruin trips.
3. **Insulation** — extra warm layer beyond what you're wearing; weather changes faster than your forecast.
4. **Illumination** — headlamp (hands-free), spare batteries. Even on day hikes, you can come out after dark.
5. **First-aid supplies** — small kit with bleeding control, blister care, medications, gloves.
6. **Fire** — lighter plus matches in a waterproof container, plus a small bag of dry tinder.
7. **Repair kit + tools** — duct tape, multi-tool, cord, safety pins, super glue. (See gear-fixes section.)
8. **Nutrition** — extra food beyond what the trip requires; an extra meal at minimum.
9. **Hydration** — water plus a way to purify more in the field.
10. **Emergency shelter** — emergency blanket or bivy, a small tarp, or both. Light and small; never weigh more than they need to.

## Scale to the trip
For a 1-hour walk near a road, this might be a small belt pouch with a phone, snack, headlamp, and emergency blanket. For a long alpine day hike, full-size versions of every item. For multi-day, expand to actual sleeping bag, tent, stove, etc. The principle is the same: the basic categories don't change, only the size of the items in them.

## Bring them every time
Half the value of the Ten Essentials is in the discipline of always carrying them, even on trips you "know" you don't need them. The unplanned situations that need them are by definition unexpected. Keep a pre-packed day kit so you grab it on the way out the door without thinking; refresh the contents quarterly.
