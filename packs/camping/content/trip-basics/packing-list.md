# Building a Pack List

## Use a written checklist
Never pack from memory. A written checklist organized by category catches forgotten items. Lay everything out in your living room before packing. Check off items as they go in. Updates the list after each trip — items you didn't use can come off, items you wished you had go on. Free apps (LighterPack) let you weigh and revise gear lists between trips.

## Core categories
Shelter (tent or tarp, footprint, stakes, repair). Sleep (bag, pad, pillow, liner if cold). Cooking (stove, fuel, pot, spork, ignition). Water (treatment, bottles, bladder). Clothing (base layers, insulation, shell, rain, hat, gloves, socks). Navigation (map, compass, GPS or phone). First aid (per length and risk of trip). Repair (small kit). Emergency (whistle, mirror, ten essentials, headlamp + spare batteries). Toiletries (toothbrush, sunscreen, lip balm, biodegradable soap, hand sanitizer, trowel, TP). Personal items (ID, keys, phone, charging if applicable).

## Climate-specific additions
Cold: heavier sleeping bag, more insulation layers, hand and foot warmers, thermos, extra fuel, beanie, balaclava, mittens, gaiters. Hot: wide-brim hat, sun shirt, electrolytes, extra water capacity, light-colored clothing, sun gloves. Wet: extra socks, dry bags for everything, rain pants, pack cover. High altitude: lip balm, sunglasses + spare, sunscreen, headlamp with spare batteries (long nights), more food calories.

## Trip-specific gear
Some additions: bear canister or hang kit (bear country), trekking poles (long descents or river crossings), bear spray (grizzly country), gaiters (snow or scree), microspikes/crampons (icy trails), helmet (rockfall or scrambling), climbing gear (technical routes), insect repellent and head net (mosquito season), satellite messenger (remote trip), water shoes (river crossings), warm hat (any night below 50°F).

## What NOT to pack
Cotton in cold/wet conditions (jeans, t-shirts, cotton hoodies) — wet cotton kills. Single-use plastics where reusables work. Heavy paperback books — a phone or Kindle if you need entertainment. Glass containers in the backcountry. Excessive duplicate items (one knife, not three). Anything you haven't used or aren't sure how to use. New gear you haven't field-tested. Strong fragrances (perfume, scented soaps) — they attract animals and irritate companions.

## Weight discipline
Standard 3-season backpacking with no luxuries: 25–35 lb total pack weight (including water and 3 days food). Lightweight: 18–25 lb. Ultralight: under 18 lb with skills and quality gear. Heavier is fine if you're conditioned for it, but heavier packs cause more injuries (knees, hips, ankles) and slower miles. Weigh your pack before leaving. If it's heavier than expected, remove items, not necessities.
