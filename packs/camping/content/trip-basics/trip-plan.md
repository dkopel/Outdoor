# Leave a Trip Plan With Someone Reliable

The single most important thing you can do before any backcountry trip is tell a trusted person where you're going, when you'll be back, and what to do if you don't return. Without this, a missing-person search may start days late — if at all.

## What goes in a trip plan
At minimum:
- **Names** of everyone in your group, with phone numbers.
- **Destination(s)**: park or forest, specific trailhead, route description.
- **Itinerary**: planned campsites, day-by-day route, alternate plans if you have them.
- **Vehicle info**: make, model, color, license plate, where you'll park it.
- **Return time and date**: "I will check in by phone by 5 PM Sunday."
- **What to do if I miss check-in**: a specific time and a specific action ("If I haven't called by Sunday 8 PM, call the [Park] dispatch at 555-1234").
- **Gear summary**: tent color, brand, distinctive packs or clothing.
- **Medical info** if relevant: allergies, conditions, medications.

## Who to give it to
Pick someone who will actually be near a phone, will remember to follow up, and is willing to make the call. A spouse, parent, or close friend works. A coworker who travels constantly is a bad choice.

## After the trip
The other half of a trip plan is closing it out. Call your contact as soon as you have signal and confirm you're out. Failing to close out a trip plan launches unnecessary searches and is unkind to both your contact and search-and-rescue volunteers.

## Sign trailhead registers
Where trailhead registers exist, fill them out fully. Searchers check these first. Write your name, party size, route, and date. On the way out, sign out — same reason as closing out a trip plan.

## Permits and reservations
Many wilderness areas require permits — and the permit office records your itinerary. This is part of your trip plan and a backup to the one you left at home. Carry your permit as proof of authorization and so rangers can identify you in the field.

## Tools that help
Personal locator beacons (PLB) and satellite communicators (Garmin inReach, Spot, etc.) let you signal emergency from anywhere. They are not a substitute for a trip plan — search teams still need to know roughly where to look — but they dramatically reduce response time. If you carry one, leave its registration info with your contact too.
