# Cottonmouth (Water Moccasin)

## Range and habitat
The cottonmouth is the only venomous water snake in the US, ranging across the southeastern coastal plain from Virginia through Florida and west to eastern Texas, plus the Mississippi River valley up to southern Illinois. They live in slow-moving water — swamps, marshes, drainage ditches, lake margins, stream backwaters — and bask on logs, banks, and overhanging branches.

## Identification
Adults 2–4 feet, heavy-bodied. Color varies: dark brown to nearly black in adults (juveniles brightly patterned with bands). Distinct triangular head, vertical pupils, pit between eye and nostril. The defining defense display: when threatened, they open the mouth wide to show the cotton-white interior — hence the name. Tail blunt and tapered, often held with the tip vibrating in dry grass (mimicking a rattle).

## Distinguishing from harmless water snakes
Many harmless water snakes share their habitat and get killed by people misidentifying them. Key differences:
- **Cottonmouth swims with body partly out of water** (buoyant due to lung position); harmless water snakes (Nerodia species) swim with most of body submerged.
- **Cottonmouths typically stand their ground or display open mouth when approached**; harmless water snakes flee or dive.
- **Vertical pupils** (cottonmouth) vs round pupils (harmless) — though you usually don't want to get that close.
- **Triangular head** vs more oval/round head on water snakes.

Without high confidence, assume any snake in southeastern wetlands could be cottonmouth and back away.

## Behavior
Cottonmouths have a reputation for aggression that's somewhat exaggerated — they prefer to display threats (open mouth, vibrating tail) rather than strike. But they're more willing to stand their ground than most US snakes, and they're frequently in places where humans wade or step (mud, water, bankside vegetation). Bites happen most when people wade into shallow water without looking, reach down for tackle or gear, or step over logs.

## Venom and symptoms
Hemotoxic venom similar to copperhead but more potent and in larger volumes. Severe pain, rapid swelling, bruising, sometimes serious tissue necrosis. Systemic symptoms include weakness, nausea, headache, occasionally bleeding disorders. Fatalities are rare but serious tissue and limb damage is real. Children and pets at higher risk.

## Field response
Same general pit viper protocol (see wildlife.rattlesnakes):
- Get away from the snake — out of the water if applicable.
- Sit, calm, still.
- Remove jewelry/tight clothing from bitten area.
- Keep limb at heart level.
- Mark swelling, note time, re-mark every 15 minutes.
- Get to a hospital with antivenom.
- Do NOT cut, suck, ice, apply tourniquet, give alcohol.

## Avoiding bites
Watch your step in southeastern wetlands. Wear tall waterproof boots when wading in shallow water; don't wade barefoot or in sandals. Use a stick or paddle to probe before stepping over logs or into thick vegetation. Don't reach into hollow logs, under banks, or into dense brush. When fishing, watch where you swing line and hooks — snakes sometimes shelter under overhanging vegetation. Keep dogs leashed.

## In a boat
Cottonmouths sometimes climb into boats or fall from overhanging branches. If a snake drops into your boat, stay calm — strike against the snake with a paddle from a distance to nudge it overboard, or move to the opposite end and let it go on its own. Don't panic; many bites happen when people thrash in a swaying boat. If you flip the boat, evacuate immediately — wet, panicked snake-in-boat scenarios go badly.
