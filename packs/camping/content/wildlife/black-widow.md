# Black Widow Spider

## Range
Three Latrodectus species in the US: southern black widow (L. mactans) across the Southeast and Southwest; northern black widow (L. variolus) in the Northeast and upper Midwest; western black widow (L. hesperus) across the Western US. Combined, they cover the entire continental US except parts of Alaska. They prefer dark, undisturbed spaces: woodpiles, sheds, outhouses, basements, rock crevices, dense brush.

## Identification
Adult female (the dangerous one): shiny black body about 1/2 inch long, with a bright red hourglass marking on the underside of the abdomen. Body globular and shiny. Legs long, slender, black. Males are much smaller, brown-tan, and harmless to humans. Juvenile females are striped and lighter colored, gradually darkening with age. The hourglass marking is the key field ID.

## Behavior and bites
Black widows are not aggressive — they bite defensively when pressed against skin. Most bites occur when reaching into a dark space (woodpiles, gloves, boots) where a widow has set up. The web is irregular, three-dimensional, often near the ground. The bite itself feels like a sharp pinprick, sometimes barely noticed.

## Symptoms
The first 30–60 minutes: local sharp pain at the bite, mild swelling, two small puncture marks (often not visible). Within 1–3 hours: muscle cramping spreading from the bite — severe abdominal cramping is classic and often misdiagnosed as appendicitis. Sweating, restlessness, anxiety, headache, nausea, vomiting, sometimes high blood pressure. Severe envenomations can cause seizures or respiratory difficulty, particularly in children and elderly.

## Treatment
Wash the bite. Apply cold compress for pain. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever (per label). Evacuate to medical care if symptoms are severe or developing: severe muscle cramping, signs of systemic illness, age extremes (very young or older adults), pregnancy, known cardiac issues. Most healthy adults recover with supportive care over 3–7 days; antivenom (Lyovac Antivenin) is available but reserved for severe cases due to risk of allergic reactions.

## Pain management at home (for milder bites)
Cold compress, rest, monitored watch. Pain peaks in 24–48 hours and gradually resolves over a week. Muscle relaxants and stronger pain medication require medical evaluation. Most importantly: monitor for worsening — most "mild" bites turn out fine but a small percentage develop severe symptoms that warrant hospital care.

## Avoiding bites
Wear gloves when moving wood, lifting rocks, cleaning shed corners, or working in undisturbed storage. Shake out boots, gloves, and clothing left outside or in storage before wearing. Inspect outhouses before sitting. Don't reach blindly into dark recesses. In high-widow areas (especially the Southwest), check outhouse seats and corners with a flashlight.

## In camp
Look in the corners of your tent vestibule before stowing gear. Check shoes left outside the tent before putting them on. If you find a black widow web with the spider in it, you can move it gently with a stick at distance, or relocate the spider. Don't kill it with bare hands.
