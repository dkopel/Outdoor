# If a Bear Attacks

The right response depends on the species and the type of attack. Bear identification matters here even more than in a typical sighting. This is reference only — read the bear identification section before you go into bear country, and carry bear spray.

## Grizzly (brown) bear — defensive attack
A defensive grizzly attack usually happens when you have surprised the bear at close range, gotten between a sow and her cubs, or approached a carcass. Leave your pack on (it protects your spine) and play dead. Lie flat on your stomach with your hands clasped behind your neck. Spread your legs wide to make it harder for the bear to roll you over. Stay completely still and silent. Remain in position until the bear has clearly left the area — playing dead too briefly can re-trigger the attack.

If the grizzly attack persists or becomes prolonged, fight back. Use any weapon at hand and focus blows on the bear's face, eyes, and muzzle.

## Black bear — defensive attack
Do **not** play dead with an attacking black bear. Try to escape to a secure place such as a vehicle or building. If escape is not possible, fight back aggressively. Concentrate kicks and strikes on the bear's face and muzzle. Use trekking poles, rocks, sticks, knives, or your fists.

## Predatory attack (any species)
If a bear of either species stalks you, attacks you in your tent, or shows other predatory behavior (focused, silent approach without warning signs), do **not** play dead. Fight back immediately with everything you have. A bear treating you as prey will not be deterred by submission.

## Bear spray during an attack
If you have bear spray and a clear shot, deploy it before the bear makes contact. Aim for the bear's face from 25 to 30 feet away (the spray creates a cloud, you don't need precise aim). After spraying, do not run — move sideways to leave the cloud.

This is general guidance for backcountry travelers. If you are mauled, even briefly, treat all wounds as serious and evacuate to professional medical care immediately. Bear bites and claw injuries carry a high infection risk.
