# Moose Encounters

More people are injured by moose each year in North America than by bears. Moose are large (up to 1,500 pounds) and fast, and they will defend themselves aggressively when they feel threatened. The good news: moose responses are very different from bear responses — you can run from a moose.

## When moose are most dangerous
A cow with a calf is the most dangerous scenario. Cow moose vigorously defend calves and will charge anyone they perceive as a threat, including hikers, dogs, and other animals. Bull moose during the autumn rut are also unpredictable. Winter moose are stressed by deep snow and limited forage and may charge to defend feeding areas.

## Warning signs before a charge
Watch the moose's body language. A relaxed moose stands with ears up and shows little interest in you. An agitated moose shows:
- Ears laid back flat against the head
- Hackles (the long hair on the neck and shoulder hump) raised
- Lip-licking, head-bobbing, or stomping front hooves
- Direct staring at you
- Lowered head, pinned ears, and a focused approach

If you see any of these signs, increase distance immediately.

## What to do during a charge
Run. Unlike with bears or mountain lions, running from a moose is the right answer — moose almost never sustain a chase. Put a solid object between you and the moose: a tree, a large rock, a vehicle, even a parked snowmobile. Most moose charges are bluff charges meant to make you leave; they rarely follow through, and rarely chase far if they do.

## If a moose knocks you down
Curl into a ball, protect your head with your arms, and stay still. Moose attack by stomping and kicking with their forelegs. Playing dead — staying small and still — signals that you are no longer a threat and the moose typically leaves. Get up only after the moose has clearly moved off.

## Reducing the risk
- Maintain at least 25 yards (about 23 m) of distance from any moose, even one that looks calm.
- Never approach calves; the cow is always nearby and watching.
- Keep dogs leashed; off-leash dogs are a leading cause of moose-on-human attacks because the dog provokes the moose and runs back to its owner.
- In winter, give moose extra room — energy stress lowers their tolerance.
- Carry bear spray; it works on moose too.
