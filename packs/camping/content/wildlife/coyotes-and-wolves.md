# Coyotes, Wolves, and Foxes

## Coyotes
Coyotes are now in every state including suburbs and cities. Most coyote encounters with humans pass without incident — coyotes prefer to avoid people. Aggressive encounters are most common with: solo small dogs, small children, and people feeding coyotes (deliberately or by leaving food out). Habituated urban-fringe coyotes are the most dangerous. If a coyote approaches: stand tall, make yourself large, shout loudly, throw small rocks or sticks toward (not directly at) them. Never run.

## Wolves
Wolf attacks on humans in North America are vanishingly rare; you have a far higher chance of being attacked by a domestic dog. Wolves in Yellowstone, Glacier, the Boundary Waters, parts of Idaho, Montana, Wyoming, the Cascades, Alaska, and northern Minnesota generally avoid people. They may be curious — following at distance, observing from cover — without being a threat. If a wolf approaches closer than ~50 yards, do exactly what you'd do for coyote: stand tall, make noise, group up, back away slowly without turning your back. Bear spray works on wolves.

## Foxes
Foxes are smaller and rarely a threat to adult humans, but they can carry rabies and steal food. A fox following you in daylight or approaching close is unusual behavior — could be habituated, could be sick. Never feed them, never let kids approach. If a fox is acting strange (unsteady, drooling, not afraid of humans), treat as potentially rabid (see wildlife.bats-and-rabies) and do not approach.

## Dogs and canids in the backcountry
The biggest canid problem in most US backcountry is unleashed domestic dogs — they can chase wildlife, harass livestock, and provoke wild predators that would otherwise leave the area alone. Keep your dog leashed in wildlife areas. Coyotes and wolves will attack and kill domestic dogs that wander into their territory, especially smaller dogs. A leashed dog held close is rarely targeted; a wandering dog often is.

## Living with predators long-term
Predator conservation is a real success story in the US — wolves restored to multiple states, mountain lion populations recovering, coyotes adapted to nearly every environment. The trade-off is that humans now share more landscape with predators. Don't feed wildlife, ever. Don't leave food, trash, or pet food outside. Don't approach for photos. Coexistence depends on humans keeping the animals wild — habituated animals are the ones that get killed, often after they injure a person who was feeding them.
