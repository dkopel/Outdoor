# Food Storage in Bear Country

Improperly stored food is the leading cause of bear–human conflict. A bear that gets a human food reward becomes a "food-conditioned" bear and is likely to escalate to dangerous behavior and eventual lethal removal. Proper food storage protects bears and people.

## What counts as "food"
Anything with an odor or flavor. This includes:
- All groceries, snacks, drinks, cooking oils
- Toiletries (toothpaste, deodorant, soap, sunscreen, insect repellent)
- Trash and food packaging (even empty wrappers)
- Stove fuel and stove
- Pet food
- Cookware that touched food
- Aromatic items: lip balm, hand lotion, scented wipes

Store all of the above the same way you store food. Sleep in clothes that have not touched food and never inside your tent with toiletries or snacks.

## Bear canisters (best option, often required)
A bear canister is a hard-sided cylinder that bears cannot open. Approved canister brands (BearVault, Garcia, Wild Ideas) carry IGBC or SIBBG certification. Place the canister at least 100 feet (about 30 m) downwind from your tent on flat ground, not near cliffs or water. Many parks (Adirondacks, much of Sierra Nevada, Denali, Olympic) require canisters by regulation.

## Bear hang (PCT method)
Where canisters aren't required and trees are tall enough:
- Find a sturdy live branch at least 20 feet off the ground.
- The food bag should hang at least 12 feet from the ground, 6 feet below the branch, and 6 feet from the trunk.
- The PCT method uses a single anchor knot and a carabiner so the bag dangles with no rope to climb.
- Hang at least 100 feet downwind from your tent.

A hung bag is the minimum standard in many areas, but a determined bear can still reach a poorly hung bag. Canisters are more reliable.

## Cooking and eating
Cook and eat at least 100 feet (some areas require 200 feet) downwind from your tent. The "bear triangle" places sleeping area, cooking area, and food storage at three corners, each 100+ feet apart. Wash dishes immediately and strain food bits into your trash (which goes into the canister or bag).

## Drive-up campgrounds
In car-accessible campsites with bear lockers, use the locker for everything — including the cooler. A cooler in a car is not bear-proof; bears routinely break car windows for food. Lock the locker.

Check the specific regulations and recommendations for the area you're visiting. Park policies differ.
