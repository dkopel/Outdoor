# Moose, Elk, and Bison

## Why ungulates injure more people than predators
Large herbivores — moose, elk, bison — injure more park visitors every year than bears or mountain lions combined. They look placid, people approach for photos, and an animal that weighs as much as a car becomes defensive at close range. Moose in rut (fall) and cow moose with calves (spring/early summer) are especially aggressive. Bison are surprisingly fast (35 mph) over short distances and will charge perceived threats without warning.

## Keep distance
General guidance for large mammals in U.S. parks: stay at least 25 yards (23 m) from any ungulate, and 100 yards (90 m) from bears and wolves. If your presence is causing an animal to change behavior — head up, ears back, stamping, turning toward you — you are too close and need to back away. Use binoculars and a long lens, not your feet, to get closer views.

## If a moose charges
Run. This is the exception — unlike bears and mountain lions, you run from moose. Get behind a substantial tree (trees a moose can't easily knock down) or other solid obstacle. Moose charge to dislodge a threat, not to kill, and will usually break off once you're behind cover. Bull moose in rut and cow moose protecting calves are the most dangerous; both lower the head and pin back the ears as warning before charging.

## If a bison charges
Bison are too fast to outrun. Get behind a vehicle, tree, or other solid cover. If no cover is available, run to the side rather than straight away — a bison's charging line is mostly straight forward. Bison gore and roll their victims; the goal of escape is to get out of the path and behind something solid as quickly as possible.

## Avoid the situation in the first place
Read the animal's behavior at distance. A relaxed grazing moose has its head down most of the time; a stressed one keeps its head up and eyes on you. Bison wallowing, raising tails, snorting, or pawing dirt are agitated. Detour widely, even off-trail. Never get between a mother and her young or near food sources you're competing for (a bull moose feeding on willows close to a trail is in his food, not yours).
