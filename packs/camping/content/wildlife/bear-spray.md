# Bear Spray — Use and Carry

Bear spray is the most effective non-lethal defense for a charging or attacking bear. Studies in Alaska and the lower 48 have shown it stops aggressive bears more reliably than firearms in close encounters.

## Buying the right product
Buy an **EPA-registered bear deterrent spray** — not human pepper spray and not "bear repellent" body sprays. The label should say "Bear Deterrent" or "Bear Spray" and list the active ingredient as capsaicin and related capsaicinoids. Each can typically delivers 6–9 seconds of spray and ranges 25–30+ feet.

## Carry it accessibly
A can buried in your pack is useless. Carry bear spray on a chest holster, hip holster, or shoulder-strap holster where you can reach it in under two seconds without looking. Practice drawing and removing the safety clip. If you carry two cans (recommended for grizzly country or extended trips), keep one on your person and one on your pack.

## When to deploy
Deploy when a bear is charging, approaching aggressively, or has made contact. Do not spray a bear that is calmly grazing or moving away — back away yourself.

## How to deploy
1. Remove the safety clip with your thumb.
2. Aim slightly downward toward the bear's face when it is 30–60 feet away.
3. Spray a 2–3 second burst, creating a cloud the bear must run through. You do not need precise aim — the cloud is the weapon.
4. If the bear continues to advance, spray a longer burst as it gets within 15 feet.
5. After the bear retreats or stops, move sideways out of the cloud. Do not run.

## Important cautions
- Never spray it on your body, gear, tent, or food as a repellent. Capsaicin residue can actually attract bears.
- Do not test-fire it near camp — wind carries the cloud unpredictably.
- Check the expiration date. Replace expired cans.
- Bear spray is restricted in checked airline luggage and prohibited in carry-on. Buy at your destination.
- Cold weather reduces propellant pressure; warm the can against your body in winter.

This is general guidance. Practice with an inert training can before relying on bear spray in the backcountry.
