# Elk

## Range and habitat
Wild elk in the Rocky Mountains (CO, WY, MT, ID, NM), Pacific Northwest (WA, OR), parts of California, Arizona, Utah, and reintroduced populations in PA, KY, NC, VA, AR, TN, MI, WI. Bulls are 700–1,000 pounds with massive antlers (in rut, late summer through fall); cows 500–600 pounds. Habitat: meadows, forest edges, mountain valleys. Towns surrounded by elk habitat (Estes Park CO, Mammoth Hot Springs WY) have elk wandering downtown daily.

## Behavior by season
**Spring/summer**: cows protect calves aggressively. Bulls grow new antlers, generally less aggressive than during rut. Mid-summer is calving season — extra care around any meadow with cows.

**Fall (September–November) — the rut**: bulls bugling, fighting, and herding cows. This is the dangerous season. Bulls in rut attack cars, fences, hikers, anything that intrudes on their cow harem or their personal space. The bugle call is loud and distinctive — if you hear bugling, slow down and assess before moving forward.

**Winter**: large herds congregate in valley wintering grounds. Less aggressive but stressed by cold and limited food; don't push them off feeding.

## NPS distance rule
At least 25 yards (23 m) from elk, all year. If an elk stops feeding to watch you, you're too close. Signs of imminent charge: head lowered with antlers forward (bulls), ears pinned back, snorting, pawing, lip curl, walking toward you with stiff legs. These are seconds-from-charge warnings.

## In rut, in town
Estes Park, Banff, Jasper, and similar towns have habituated elk that lounge on lawns and golf courses. Don't take selfies. Don't walk between a bull and his cows. Don't approach calves. Bulls have attacked people in their yards, charged trucks, and damaged buildings during rut. Local rangers and police regularly use bean-bag rounds and other deterrents on belligerent rut bulls.

## Elk charges
Same general response as bison: get behind solid cover, run perpendicular to the charge line not straight away, climb if needed (large tree, vehicle, structure). Elk are slightly less massive than bison but have antlers (bulls) and cloven hooves that can severely injure. The rut bull's goal is to displace what he perceives as a rival or threat — once you're well away, he usually breaks off.

## If gored or kicked
Bulls gore with antler tines and kick with front and hind hooves. Injuries are typically punctures and deep bruises, sometimes broken bones. Treat for bleeding control, suspected fractures, shock. Evacuate to medical care. Tetanus shot consideration if not current.

## Photography
Use a telephoto lens. Stay in or near your vehicle. Don't approach. Don't try to provoke a bugle reaction (calling, whistling, mimicking). The most spectacular elk photos are taken from vehicles or distant blinds with proper lenses — not from 10 feet away with a phone.
