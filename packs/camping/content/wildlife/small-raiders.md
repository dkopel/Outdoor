# Small Food Raiders

Bears are the headline wildlife concern, but raccoons, marmots, ground squirrels, mice, and chipmunks are far more likely to chew through your pack or tent looking for food. They are also harder to deter because they can get into smaller spaces and chew through fabric.

## Threats from small raiders
- **Raccoons** in campgrounds will open coolers, unzip packs, and remember which campers are careless. They are intelligent and dexterous.
- **Marmots** in alpine zones chew on rubber, leather, and sweat-soaked gear (boot insoles, pack straps, hiking poles) for the salt. Vehicle brake lines and hoses are also a known target in some park parking lots.
- **Mice and chipmunks** chew through tent mesh and pack fabric to reach food. A single mouse can ruin a pack overnight.
- **Deer and goats** in some parks are habituated to human salt — sweat-soaked clothing and even urine puddles attract them. In rare cases mountain goats have attacked people for salt.

## Prevention
- Use a hard-sided container for food when possible. Soft bags are easily chewed.
- Hang anything chewable, not just food. Pack straps with sweat residue are a target.
- In marmot country, do not leave hiking poles, hats, gloves, or boots outside your tent overnight. Hang them or bring them inside.
- Park in designated areas; marmots and porcupines chew vehicle hoses in some lots — wrap with chicken wire if locally recommended.
- Don't pee within 100 feet of camp or trail; salty soil attracts wildlife to high-use areas.
- Keep packs zipped fully when not in use.

## If a raid happens
A raid is not a wildlife emergency, but report it to rangers — habituated animals require management response. Repair holes in tents or packs as soon as possible; the smell of food residue persists and attracts more raiders.

## Health note
Some small mammals carry hantavirus, plague, and other zoonoses. Do not touch dead rodents with bare hands. Wash up after any contact.
