# Alligators and Crocodiles

## Where they live
American alligators range across the southeastern US: Florida, Louisiana, Georgia, South Carolina, Alabama, Mississippi, and parts of Texas, Arkansas, North Carolina, and Oklahoma. They live in fresh and slightly brackish water — swamps, marshes, rivers, lakes, golf course ponds. American crocodiles live only in extreme southern Florida (Everglades, Florida Keys), in saltier coastal water. Both can be present in canals, retention ponds, and waterways near campgrounds throughout these areas.

## Don't approach
Adult alligators are 8–14 feet long and 500+ pounds, and can short-burst sprint to 30 mph for 10–20 feet. They feed by ambush, lunging from water onto banks. Stay at least 50–60 feet from any alligator. The shoreline of any southeastern body of water is potential alligator habitat — assume one could be there even if you don't see it. Don't approach for photos. Don't dangle feet, hands, or pet leashes near the water's edge. Don't let dogs swim or walk close to the water in alligator country.

## Feeding is the worst thing
Never feed alligators. Fed alligators lose fear of humans, associate humans with food, and the resulting attacks are usually fatal. It's illegal in every state where alligators live. Fishermen cleaning fish at the water's edge are a leading cause of alligator-human conflict; clean fish away from the water and dispose of remains properly. Habituated alligators are routinely killed by wildlife managers — feeding them is a death sentence for the animal too.

## If one approaches
Back away slowly and steadily. Never turn your back. Distance is your friend; an alligator is unlikely to chase you 30 yards across dry ground if you keep moving. Get to high ground or behind cover. If an alligator hisses at you or moves toward you, leave the area entirely; that animal has already decided you're a threat to defend against, and may be guarding a nest.

## In the water
The most dangerous places are: dusk through dawn (alligators feed most actively), shallow water with overhanging vegetation, near nesting areas. Don't swim in any natural water in alligator country unless it's posted as safe. Don't wade in canal-style waterways. Boating: keep dogs and kids away from the gunwales; alligators have grabbed pets out of small boats. Falling overboard in alligator water is a real risk; wear a PFD.

## If attacked
Fight aggressively. Punch, gouge eyes, hit the snout, push fingers into the nostrils. Alligators rely on their bite — if you make the attack costly enough, many will release. Don't play dead. Once free, get away from the water as fast as possible. Bites are typically severe wounds with high infection risk (alligator mouths are full of bacteria); evacuate to medical care immediately even if injuries seem minor. Tourniquet for limb bites where bleeding can't be controlled.
